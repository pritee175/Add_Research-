# Detecting Tobacco/Alcohol/Panmasala-Gutka Products vs. Classifying Promotional Intent: State of the Art

## What CV/NLP methods exist for detecting tobacco/cigarette/alcohol/panmasala/gutka products, logos, or brand cues in images, video frames, or audio/text?

### Takeaway
A substantial body of CV/NLP work exists for detecting the *presence* of tobacco, alcohol, e-cigarette, and (to a much lesser extent) smokeless-tobacco/panmasala products, logos, and use-behaviors in images, video, and text — using CNNs, object detectors (YOLO, Faster/Mask R-CNN), and transformer-based text/vision-language models. Coverage of panmasala/gutka specifically is essentially absent in the CV/NLP literature; the closest matches are medical-imaging papers on oral-cancer detection in gutka users, not product/ad detection.

### Cited Findings
- A deep-learning cigarette-in-image recognition pipeline using CNN-based object detection to tag images containing smoking activity is described in "Image Analytics to Detect Cigarette in an Image Using Deep Learning" (Springer, 2021) — [Source](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-8391-9_48)
- Features extracted via Inception V3 convolutional layers trained a classifier that detects cigarettes in video frames with 89.99% accuracy, applied to automated censoring of cigarettes in videos — [Source](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-3643-4_26) (~2020)
- "Image Processing for Public Health Surveillance of Tobacco Point-of-Sale Advertising: Machine Learning–Based Methodology" (English et al., JMIR, 27 Aug 2021): used a pretrained Inception V3 network with a custom classification head for brand classification, and YOLOv3 (Darknet-53 backbone) for logo localization, across 42 tobacco brands (cigarettes, cigars, smokeless tobacco/snus, e-cigarettes) collected from 86,683 West Virginia (2013–2014) and 13,264 Washington DC (2018) point-of-sale photos; achieved >75% classification accuracy for 8/42 brands (best: Copenhagen 90.4%, Winston 85.1%, Pyramid 82.7%), mean average precision 0.72 and IoU 0.62 for Marlboro logo localization. Explicitly scoped to presence/location detection only, not promotional messaging — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8433867/)
- "Scalable Surveillance of E-Cigarette Products on Instagram and TikTok Using Computer Vision" (Vassey, Kennedy, Chang, Smith, Unger; Nicotine & Tobacco Research, 2023/2024): trained a DyHead object detector with a Swin-Large Transformer backbone on 6,999 annotated Instagram images across 8 object classes (mod/pod devices, e-juice containers, packaging, warning labels, brand names, flavor names, smoke clouds); applied to 14,072 promotional TikTok videos (2019–2022; ~10.3M frames). mAP ranged 0.46 (smoke clouds) to 0.89 (e-juice containers/flavor names); found pod-device prevalence rose 15% and brand/flavor visibility doubled 2019–2022. The authors explicitly state the model "was not trained to identify more nuanced themes, for example, instructional versus promotional videos" — [Source](https://academic.oup.com/ntr/article/26/5/552/7382253)
- Hookah/waterpipe detection on Instagram used a CNN feature extractor + SVM classifier to distinguish waterpipe vs. non-waterpipe images, reaching 99.5% accuracy (2018/arXiv) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08881)
- Large-cigar detection on Reddit used YOLOv7 on 876 large-cigar and 1,526 non-cigar images, achieving recall 0.98, precision 0.99, F1 0.98, and 97% accuracy distinguishing cigars from visually similar objects (PubMed, 2025) — [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41081730/)
- "Application Based Cigarette Detection on Social Media Platforms Using Machine Learning Algorithms" (Springer, 2022) applies ML-based cigarette detection to social platform images — [Source](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-91387-8_5)
- "A multimodal deep learning architecture for smoking detection with a small data approach" (Lakatos, Pollner, Hajdu, Joó; 2024, posted to medRxiv/PMC and arXiv): used CLIP-ViT-B-32 for multimodal filtering + fine-tuned EfficientNet B5 for image classification (74% accuracy), and XLM-RoBERTa NER on a ChatGPT-generated Hungarian text corpus (80 paragraphs, ~49,000 characters) for text classification (98% accuracy, 91% F1); explicit goal was "detecting hidden advertising" and quantifying tobacco-related media content, i.e., oriented toward presence/exposure quantification, not an explicit promote/discourage label — [Source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10936563/)
- "SmokEng: Towards Fine-grained Classification of Tobacco-related Social Media Text" (arXiv, 2019) performs fine-grained NLP classification of tobacco-related text posts (product type / use context), a text-side analogue to image brand/product detection — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.05598)
- A pretrained, no-training-required "alcohol use identifier" image classifier (binary has-alcohol/no-alcohol) is offered commercially by Nyckel for content-moderation use cases — [Source](https://www.nyckel.com/pretrained-classifiers/alcohol-use-identifier/)
- Twitter-based alcohol surveillance filtered tweets using drinking-related keywords ("drunk," "beer," "party") to detect geo-temporal alcohol consumption patterns (arXiv, 2016) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.03181)
- A segmentation study annotated 1,333 smoking images and benchmarked Mask R-CNN, Cascade Mask R-CNN, and Hybrid Task Cascade (HTC) for smoking-image detection (per search aggregation, exact source paper not independently verified beyond search snippet) — Gap noted below.
- No panmasala/gutka *product or advertisement* detection system was found. The only gutka-adjacent deep-learning work located is "Initial Stage Oral Cancer Symptom Prediction of Tobacco and Gutka Consumers Using Deep Learning Method: A Case Study on India," which applies ResNet-50/EfficientNetB0 transfer learning to oral-cavity photographs (500 cancerous vs. 400 non-cancerous images, ResNet-50 training accuracy 98.66%) to detect oral cancer symptoms in gutka users — this is a medical-diagnostic application, not product/brand/ad detection — [Source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385811726_Initial_Stage_Oral_Cancer_Symptom_Prediction_of_Tobacco_and_Gutka_Consumers_Using_Deep_Learning_Method_A_Case_Study_on_India)

### Inferences
- The field has mature, reusable building blocks (YOLO-family detectors, CLIP/transformer multimodal encoders, fine-tuned CNN classifiers) for presence/brand/logo detection of tobacco and alcohol products across images, video, and text, with reported accuracies often in the high-80s to high-90s% range on narrow, curated datasets.
- Panmasala/gutka — a product category of central importance in the Indian context — has essentially no dedicated CV/NLP detection literature; any Indian-focused system would likely need to build brand/logo datasets from scratch (the closest transferable methodology is the POS tobacco-brand pipeline of English et al. 2021).

### Gaps
- Could not verify the Mask R-CNN/Cascade Mask-R-CNN/HTC smoking-segmentation study's authorship, year, or dataset source beyond the aggregated search snippet; full citation not confirmed.
- No audio-based (e.g., spoken-mention-in-ad-audio) detection system for these product categories was found in the time available; this modality appears largely unaddressed in the literature.
- No panmasala/gutka-specific brand/logo/advertisement CV dataset or detector was found, Indian or otherwise.

## Has anyone built a classifier that distinguishes "promoting" vs "discouraging/warning against" use of these products in ads, PSAs, or movie/TV content? (any country)

### Takeaway
A small but growing number of studies explicitly attempt the promote-vs-discourage/stance distinction — almost entirely in **text** (social media posts) rather than image/video, and almost entirely for **tobacco/vaping**, not alcohol or panmasala/gutka; true multimodal promotional-intent classifiers for ads/PSAs/movie-TV content are rare to nonexistent, and the one video-domain system found explicitly disclaims this capability.

### Cited Findings
- "AI for Tobacco Control: Identifying Tobacco-Promoting Social Media Content Using Large Language Models" (Küçükali & Erdoğan, Nicotine & Tobacco Research, 2024/2025) is the clearest hit on this exact task: they fine-tuned BERTurk (Turkish BERT) on 177,684 Turkish tweets (October 2020), with 5,000 tweets manually annotated (4,498 after profanity filtering, 3,575 with consistent dual-reviewer agreement across 27 volunteer coders) under a 4-category framework — "favors tobacco," "against tobacco," "both," or "none." The classifier explicitly targets stance/intent (modeling tobacco-use behavior, expressing positive attitudes, recommending use, or marketing specific brands/vendors as "promoting"), achieving recall 87.8%, precision 81.1%, accuracy 81.7%, F1 84.3%, and found 39.9% of tobacco-keyword-containing tweets were tobacco-promoting — [Source](https://academic.oup.com/ntr/article/27/6/988/7907793)
- "Characterizing Anti-Vaping Posts for Effective Communication on Instagram Using Multimodal Deep Learning" (Xie, Deng, Liu, Lou, Xu, Li; Nicotine & Tobacco Research Supplement, 2024): uses CLIP (ViT-B/32) + logistic regression for image features and LDA topic modeling + VADER sentiment for captions, fused via negative binomial regression, on 8,972 anti-vaping Instagram posts (hand-coded subset of 2,200 images) collected via anti-vaping hashtags (#novape, #stopvaping, #antivaping). Important caveat: **this study does not itself classify promoting-vs-discouraging intent** — it assumes all posts are already anti-vaping (by hashtag selection) and instead predicts which anti-vaping message *features* (e.g., warning signs vs. showing a person vaping) drive higher audience engagement — [Source](https://academic.oup.com/ntr/article/26/Supplement_1/S43/7608652)
- The e-cigarette CV surveillance paper (Vassey et al., cited above) explicitly flags that distinguishing "instructional versus promotional videos" is a known unmet need beyond their object-presence detection — direct evidence of the presence-detection-only gap — [Source](https://academic.oup.com/ntr/article/26/5/552/7382253)
- A U.S. NCI monograph chapter, "Role of Entertainment Media in Promoting or Discouraging Tobacco Use" (Monograph 19, National Cancer Institute, cancercontrol.cancer.gov), frames the promote-vs-discourage distinction conceptually for entertainment media content, but based on available material this is a narrative/public-health-policy synthesis, not an automated ML classifier — treat as conceptual precedent only, not a technical system — [Source](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/m19_10.pdf)
- No peer-reviewed system was found that classifies movie/TV/ad **video or image** content as promoting vs. discouraging tobacco/alcohol/panmasala use using CV (as opposed to manual human content-coding, which is the norm — see next section).
- No promote-vs-discourage classifier of any kind (text, image, or video) for **alcohol** or **panmasala/gutka** content was found in this search.

### Inferences
- The promote-vs-discourage distinction has been operationalized successfully only as a **text classification** task on social media (tobacco/vaping), via supervised fine-tuning of a language model against a manually double-coded ground truth — suggesting that a project targeting *video/image* promotional-intent classification (e.g., Indian ads, PSAs, films) would be extending a validated text paradigm into a largely unexplored visual/multimodal space.
- Even within the one true promote/discourage classifier found (Küçükali & Erdoğan 2024), the "marketing specific brands/vendors" signal is closest to what Indian regulators call surrogate/promotional advertising, suggesting the coding scheme could be adapted to ads/PSAs if a comparable labeled corpus were built.
- The anti-vaping Instagram study is frequently discoverable under search terms suggesting it does stance classification, but closer reading shows it answers a different question (what drives engagement with already-known-anti content) — this is an important nuance for scoping a related-work section and for avoiding over-claiming novelty against it.

### Gaps
- No study was found that applies NLP/CV promotional-intent classification to **advertisements or PSAs specifically** (as opposed to organic social media posts) for any of these product categories.
- No study was found addressing promote-vs-discourage classification for **alcohol** content in any modality.
- No study was found addressing promote-vs-discourage classification for **panmasala/gutka** content in any modality.
- No Indian-context automated promote-vs-discourage classifier (text, image, or video) was found at all — this appears to be a genuine, confirmed gap in the literature as currently searchable.

## What techniques have been used for detecting on-screen tobacco use in films, and do they address intent/framing?

### Takeaway
Film/TV tobacco-depiction surveillance is a well-established public-health research area, but it relies overwhelmingly on **manual human content-coding** protocols (notably the "Breathe California" / Glantz coding method), not automated CV; where automated/ML approaches exist, they detect presence (cigarette objects, smoking gestures) and are not used to assess narrative framing, glamorization, or health-warning compliance — researchers explicitly flag automation as a future need, not a current capability.

### Cited Findings
- The standard methodology, developed and used by Stanton Glantz and colleagues and tracked as a CDC "core surveillance indicator," defines a "tobacco incident" as "the use or implied use of a tobacco product by an actor" onscreen, with a new incident counted when the product goes off/on screen, a different actor appears with it, or the scene changes; coding is done by ≥2 independent human monitors with disagreements resolved by a supervisor — [Source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3498945/)
- CDC has compiled Glantz's top-grossing-movie tobacco-incident data since 1991; a 2011 update (published in *Preventing Chronic Disease*) found tobacco depictions per youth-rated movie rebounded, up more than one-third from 2010 — [Source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5726239/) (general area; exact PCD citation not independently re-verified)
- "Tobacco Imagery in Movies and Web Series Streaming in India and Their Compliance With the Recent Anti-tobacco Rules for Over-the-Top (OTT) Platforms" (Jena et al., 2024): 7 trained human coders applied the Breathe California method to 29 movies and 31 web series across 7 Indian OTT platforms (Oct–Nov 2023), manually identifying 237 tobacco incidents (avg. 3.95/show). Compliance findings: **zero** compliance with health-spot/audio-visual warning requirements across all shows; only 40% showed the audio-visual disclaimer (and only at the beginning); only 35.7% of shows with tobacco imagery fully complied with static on-screen warning-message requirements (movies 57.1% vs. web series 14.3%). The paper explicitly recommends that "AI technology, when available, may also be used for real-time monitoring of tobacco imagery in OTT shows" — confirming no automated system currently exists for this compliance-monitoring task in India — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998687/)
- The same Breathe California coding method was applied to 240 top-grossing Bollywood films (2006–2017) to study tobacco-depiction trends before/after India's Tobacco-Free Film and TV Rules, using Poisson regression on manually coded incident counts — WHO SEARO-hosted PDF (Tobacco Control journal) — [Source](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/searo/india/health-topic-pdf/tobacco/changes-in-tobacco-depictions-after-implementation-of-tobacco-free-film-and-tv-rules-_tobacco-control.pdf)
- A related WHO SEARO report, "Tobacco imagery in on-demand streaming content popular among adolescents and young adults in India" (2020), covers OTT tobacco depiction prevalence (manual methodology, consistent with other entries in this cluster) — [Source](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/searo/india/health-topic-pdf/tobacco/tobaccocontrol-tobacco-imagery-in-on-demand-ott-india-2020.pdf)
- On the automated side, "Development of framework for detecting smoking scene in video clips" proposes using TensorFlow's Object Detection API to detect cigarette objects in video clips in order to automatically trigger warning-message display — a presence-detection/compliance-automation tool, not an intent/framing classifier (ResearchGate, methodology-focused paper, exact year/authors not independently confirmed) — [Source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330159436_Development_of_framework_for_detecting_smoking_scene_in_video_clips)
- "Automated Censoring of Cigarettes in Videos Using Deep Learning Techniques" (Inception V3 features, 89.99% accuracy) is explicitly aimed at censorship/compliance automation (detect-and-blur), again presence-only — [Source](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-3643-4_26)
- India's regulatory requirements (COTPA / CBFC rules) mandate editorial justification, a 20-second anti-tobacco disclaimer, a 30-second anti-tobacco ad before the film and at intermission, and a static on-screen health warning whenever tobacco is depicted — these rules presuppose binary tobacco-depiction detection but say nothing about classifying whether the depiction itself is glamorizing/promotional vs. cautionary — [Source](https://cbfcindia.gov.in/cbfcAdmin/assets/pdf/COTPA.pdf)
- Vital Strategies' evaluation of the Tobacco-Free Film and TV Rules found the health-warning static message was accurately shown in only 19% of films, and shown in 65% of TV content with tobacco scenes but the static message was improperly displayed in all of those instances — reinforcing that compliance checking in India today is manual/audit-based, not automated — [Source](https://www.vitalstrategies.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/VS_ExecSummary_FilmRuleStudy02.pdf)

### Inferences
- There is a strong, decades-long public-health research tradition (Glantz/Breathe California method, CDC indicator, WHO SEARO/India studies) of coding tobacco *presence* in film/TV, but it is entirely human-labor-based; none of the found studies code for narrative framing (e.g., is the depiction glamorizing, villain-associated, comedic, or cautionary) using automated methods, and all explicitly or implicitly treat automation of *presence detection and compliance checking* (not intent/framing classification) as the frontier still to be reached.
- This is a direct, explicit confirmation of the research gap motivating a new project: multiple papers (Jena et al. 2024 on India OTT; Vassey et al. 2023 on e-cigarette TikTok) independently flag that current tools detect presence/objects but not promotional framing or intent, and recommend AI for monitoring but not for framing classification.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm exact authorship/year for the "Development of framework for detecting smoking scene in video clips" ResearchGate paper beyond the title; treat with caution.
- No study was found that attempts automated classification of *glamorization* or *narrative valence* of on-screen tobacco/alcohol/gutka use (e.g., hero vs. villain association, comedic vs. dramatic framing) in any country's film/TV corpus.

## Are there related tasks like alcohol/tobacco use detection in social media images, or ad sentiment/persuasion classification, that are methodologically close?

### Takeaway
Yes — two adjacent, more mature literatures are methodologically close and directly transferable: (1) social-media substance-use-image/text detection (CNN/transformer pipelines, well-validated), and (2) general multimodal ad-persuasion-strategy classification (attention-fusion transformer models over image+OCR text+object regions), though the latter has not been applied to tobacco/alcohol/panmasala ads or to a promote/discourage axis specifically.

### Cited Findings
- "Persuasion Strategies in Advertisements: Dataset, Modeling, and Baselines" (Yaman Kumar Singla et al., accepted AAAI-23, arXiv Aug 2022) built the first ad-image corpus annotated with a persuasion-strategy taxonomy (grounded partly in Cialdini's principles and Aristotelian rhetorical appeals), covering ads from "1600 advertising campaigns of 30 Fortune-500 companies," and proposed a multi-task attention-fusion model combining ad image, OCR-extracted text, object-detector ROIs, and image-captioning embeddings via a transformer cross-attention module. The available abstract/summary does not confirm coverage of tobacco/alcohol ads or a promotional-vs-preventive axis — this is a general commercial-persuasion framework, not health-content-specific — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09626)
- "BCAmirs at SemEval-2024 Task 4: Beyond Words: A Multimodal and Multilingual Exploration of Persuasion in Memes" demonstrates multimodal persuasion-technique classification (propaganda-style rhetorical device detection) in memes, another methodologically adjacent multimodal-persuasion task — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.03022)
- "#Vape: Measuring E-Cigarette Influence on Instagram With Deep Learning and Text Analysis" combines deep-learning image classification (distinguishing images of men, women, vaping devices) with text analysis over ~49,655 Instagram posts, an architecture directly transferable to other substance categories — [Source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8883232/)
- "The Normalization of Vaping on TikTok Using Computer Vision, Natural Language Processing, and Qualitative Thematic Analysis" (JMIR, 2024) combines CV, NLP, and qualitative thematic coding to study how vaping content is normalized on TikTok — title and framing suggest direct relevance to the promotion/normalization question, but full-text could not be retrieved in this session, so specific methods/findings cannot be cited with confidence — flagged as a high-priority follow-up read rather than a confirmed finding — [Source](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e55591)
- "Characterizing Vaping Posts on Instagram by Using Unsupervised Machine Learning" applies unsupervised clustering to vaping-related Instagram content, another methodologically adjacent approach to characterizing (not strictly promote/discourage-labeling) substance-related social content — [Source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342341470_Characterizing_Vaping_Posts_on_Instagram_by_Using_Unsupervised_Machine_Learning)
- "Automated Detection of Vaping-Related Tweets on Twitter During the 2019 EVALI Outbreak Using Machine Learning Classification" shows ML text classification applied reactively to a public-health crisis, an adjacent surveillance paradigm — [Source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8866955/)
- General surrogate-advertising scholarship (non-ML, legal/marketing literature) establishes that Indian alcohol surrogate ads use proxy products (soda, mineral water, music CDs) carrying the parent brand's name/packaging — this is a well-documented *pattern* that any future Indian detection system would need to encode as domain knowledge, since no detector currently models it — [Source](https://www.manupatra.com/roundup/330/articles/barcelona.pdf); regulatory background also at [Source](https://eucam.info/2026/03/30/walking-the-fine-line-why-india-must-close-the-surrogate-advertising-loophole/)

### Inferences
- The multimodal ad-persuasion-strategy modeling pipeline (OCR text + object ROIs + caption embeddings + cross-attention fusion, per Singla et al. 2022) is architecturally the closest published template for a future "promotional-intent classifier" for tobacco/alcohol/panmasala ads — it would need (a) a new labeled dataset specific to these product categories and the promote/discourage axis, and (b) likely a surrogate-advertising-aware feature set (brand-name/packaging similarity to banned parent brands) that no current system incorporates.
- Across nearly all the adjacent social-media literature, "detection" means object/brand/use-presence or audience-engagement prediction, not promotional-stance labeling — reinforcing that stance/intent labeling is the consistent blind spot even in otherwise mature adjacent tasks.

### Gaps
- Full text of the JMIR 2024 TikTok vaping-normalization paper could not be retrieved (fetch returned empty content twice); its specific methodology, dataset size, and findings remain unconfirmed and should be re-attempted by the report writer or in a follow-up pass if full detail is needed.
- No persuasion-strategy-classification study was found that includes tobacco, alcohol, or panmasala/gutka ads as a tested category, nor one that maps persuasion strategies onto a promote/discourage or health-message axis.

## What are the known technical limitations or failure modes of pure "presence detection" systems that motivate moving to intent classification?

### Takeaway
Multiple independent sources — both methodological surveys of logo/brand detection and the substance-specific surveillance papers themselves — explicitly document that presence/object detectors cannot distinguish context, purpose, or narrative framing, and several authors explicitly name "promotional vs. instructional/non-promotional" or "glamorizing vs. cautionary" distinctions as acknowledged, unaddressed limitations of their own systems.

### Cited Findings
- Vassey et al. (2023/2024), on their own e-cigarette object-detection system, state directly: the model "was not trained to identify more nuanced themes, for example, instructional versus promotional videos" — a first-party acknowledgment that object/brand presence detection does not yield intent classification — [Source](https://academic.oup.com/ntr/article/26/5/552/7382253)
- English et al. (2021), on their tobacco POS-advertising image pipeline, scope their system explicitly to presence/logo detection and spatial location, explicitly not analyzing "promotional messaging or targeting intent" — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8433867/)
- Jena et al. (2024), studying Indian OTT tobacco-warning compliance, note current monitoring is entirely manual and recommend future AI only for "real-time monitoring of tobacco imagery" (i.e., presence/compliance), not for assessing whether a depiction glamorizes or discourages use — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998687/)
- A survey/discussion of brand-logo detection limitations notes that: (1) pure visual systems struggle with small, partially obscured, or unusually angled logos and lack reasoning to distinguish a scene's actual advertised subject from an incidentally visible background logo (e.g., misidentifying a cereal ad as a refrigerator ad because a refrigerator brand logo is visible); (2) modern advertisers increasingly use product placement, distinctive packaging, or voiceover brand mentions rather than prominent logos, creating a gap between what logo detectors can see and how brands are actually promoted; (3) logo detectors are highly context-dependent and fail when a known logo appears in an unfamiliar context (e.g., trained on shoes, confused by the same logo on a mug); (4) maintaining such systems requires expensive re-annotation/retraining for every new brand or logo redesign — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.04399) (synthesized from multiple results in this search cluster; see also [Source](https://code.tubitv.com/adrise-brand-intelligence-how-we-use-ai-to-recognize-brands-in-ad-video-bc036aeeabf1))
- The anti-vaping Instagram engagement study (Xie et al., 2024) implicitly illustrates the presence/intent gap from the opposite direction: a post "showing a person vaping" can appear in an *anti*-vaping, hashtag-curated post, meaning presence-of-use detection alone would misclassify such content as pro-use/promotional without additional context (the hashtag/caption-level framing is what actually signals the anti-use intent) — [Source](https://academic.oup.com/ntr/article/26/Supplement_1/S43/7608652)
- Surrogate advertising in India is specifically designed to defeat presence/brand detection of the *actual* restricted product: the ad shows a legal proxy product (soda, mineral water) while promoting the alcohol brand by name/packaging resemblance — meaning a detector trained to find tobacco/alcohol logos or liquid-in-glass imagery would systematically miss the dominant real-world promotional mechanism in the Indian ad market — [Source](https://www.manupatra.com/roundup/330/articles/barcelona.pdf); regulatory enforcement is described as "inconsistent" with India reportedly drafting a blanket ban due to loophole exploitation — [Source](https://eucam.info/2026/03/30/walking-the-fine-line-why-india-must-close-the-surrogate-advertising-loophole/)

### Inferences
- The failure modes cluster into three distinct categories relevant to motivating an intent-classification project: (1) **context-insensitivity** — detectors cannot tell promotional from incidental/cautionary appearance of the same object; (2) **adversarial evasion** — real-world advertisers (especially in India, via surrogate advertising) actively design content to defeat presence-based brand/product detection, meaning presence detection is not just incomplete but actively gameable; (3) **self-acknowledged scope limits** — multiple peer-reviewed systems in this exact domain explicitly name the promotional-intent/framing problem as future work they did not attempt, which is strong direct evidence of an open research gap rather than an inferred one.
- Because surrogate advertising is a documented, legally recognized evasion strategy specific to the Indian alcohol/tobacco ad market, any Indian-context intent classifier would likely need to reason jointly over brand/packaging similarity, legal proxy-product categories, and context (e.g., broadcast timing, associated celebrity endorsement) rather than relying on product presence alone — a composite task no system reviewed here currently performs.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment was found that directly measures the error rate of a presence-only detector when applied to promotional-intent labeling (e.g., quantifying how often "shows a cigarette" incorrectly predicts "promotes smoking" on a held-out ad/PSA/film dataset) — this would be a valuable empirical baseline for a new project to establish itself, since the literature currently only asserts the limitation qualitatively rather than measuring it.
