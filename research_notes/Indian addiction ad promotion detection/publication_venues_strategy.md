# Publication Venue & Strategy Research: Dataset + Multimodal Classifier Paper on Indian Addiction-Related Ad Detection

## What are the main venues that accept "new dataset + baseline model" papers in multimodal ML/NLP/CV?

### Takeaway
There is a well-established "dataset track" ecosystem across the major ML/NLP/CV conferences (NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets, ACL-family resource papers, LREC-COLING, ACM Multimedia Dataset track), all of which explicitly welcome new annotated datasets paired with baseline models, and these are realistic, non-industry-scale targets for a first dataset+classifier paper. Exact 2026/2027 dates will shift and must be reverified close to submission, but the cyclical pattern (spring/summer conference deadlines, biennial LREC, continuous ACL Rolling Review) is stable.

### Cited Findings
- NeurIPS renamed its "Datasets & Benchmarks" track to the "Evaluations & Datasets (E&D)" track for 2026, with an expanded scope covering evaluation science as well as datasets/resources used across the ML lifecycle (training, fine-tuning, testing, auditing) — [NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations & Datasets Track CFP](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets); [Introducing the Evaluations & Datasets Track blog post](https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/03/23/introducing-the-evaluations-datasets-track-at-neurips-2026/)
- For NeurIPS 2026, E&D track deadlines matched the main/Papers-for-Position tracks: abstracts due May 4, 2026 (AoE), full papers due May 6, 2026 (AoE) — [NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations & Datasets Track CFP](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets)
- NeurIPS 2026 now requires all dataset submissions to include Responsible AI (RAI) metadata as part of the dataset's Croissant file, for transparency — [NeurIPS 2026 E&D CFP](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets)
- ACL Rolling Review (ARR) — the unified continuous review system feeding ACL/EMNLP/NAACL — explicitly includes "data resources" as an eligible scope of submission (long/short papers), and reviewers are instructed to check whether papers contributing artifacts like datasets clearly specify release terms — [ARR Call for Papers](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp); [ARR Authors Guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/authors)
- LREC-COLING (Language Resources and Evaluation Conference, biennial) is squarely focused on language-resource/dataset papers, including resource development for LLMs (monolingual, multilingual, **multimodal**), and offers a dedicated resource-sharing step (LRE Map) at submission — [LREC 2026 Calls](https://lrec2026.info/calls/); [ACL Anthology LREC venue](https://aclanthology.org/venues/lrec/)
- LREC 2026 (15th edition) is being held in Palma, Mallorca, Spain, 11–16 May 2026; for that cycle the paper deadline was 17 October 2025, notification 13 February 2026, camera-ready 15 March 2026; submissions are 4–8 pages excluding references — [LREC 2026 Calls](https://lrec2026.info/calls/)
- ACM Multimedia runs a dedicated "Call for Dataset Papers" track separate from its main technical track — [ACM MM 2026 Call for Dataset Papers](https://2026.acmmm.org/site/call-datasets.html)
- ACM MM 2026 (Rio de Janeiro, 10–14 Nov 2026) main-track deadlines: abstract Mar 25, 2026; full paper Apr 1, 2026; supplementary Apr 8, 2026; notifications Jul 7, 2026; dataset-paper full-paper deadline also Apr 1, 2026 — [ACM MM 2026 CFP guidelines](https://2026.acmmm.org/site/cfp-guidelines.html); [ACM MM 2026 Call for Dataset Papers](https://2026.acmmm.org/site/call-datasets.html)
- WACV 2026 hosts a "SAFE" (Synthetic & Adversarial ForEnsics) workshop (Mar 6, 2026) on provenance/authenticity/forensics — adjacent to, but not the same framing as, ad/health-content classification; CVPR 2026 has an "Authenticity & Provenance in the Age of AI" workshop (Jun 3, 2026) — [SAFE 2026](https://wacv.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/workshop/24); [WACV 2026 overview](https://encord.com/wacv-2026/)
- ICWSM (AAAI International Conference on Web and Social Media) — a computational social science venue — has a dedicated **Dataset submission track** with a 10-page limit, single-blind (not double-blind) anonymization because dataset papers often need to link to hosting services, and offers dataset hosting plus a best-dataset award; ICWSM-2025's dataset deadline was Jan 15, 2025 with notification Mar 15, 2025 — [ICWSM 2026 Submit page](https://www.icwsm.org/2026/submit.html); [ICWSM dataset submission details](https://www.icwsm.org/2025/submit/index.html)

### Inferences
- For a small team's first dataset+model paper, ACL/EMNLP/NAACL "resource paper" submission via ARR, ICWSM's dataset track, or LREC-COLING are lower-barrier entry points than NeurIPS E&D or ACM MM, which have grown more competitive and now carry extra documentation burdens (e.g., NeurIPS's new Croissant/RAI metadata requirement).
- Because the project is explicitly multimodal (text+image ad classification) and health-policy-adjacent, ICWSM is a particularly good fit: it is built for exactly this kind of "new data + algorithmic method applied to a social phenomenon" paper, and its single-blind/relaxed anonymization rules are friendlier to papers that must link to a dataset-hosting page.
- LREC-COLING is biennial, so the next full cycle after the one found (2026) would likely fall in 2028 — a relevant planning constraint if targeting that venue specifically for a resource paper.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm current (2026/2027-forward) NeurIPS E&D or ACM MM acceptance rates specific to the dataset sub-tracks (these are typically reported after the review cycle closes, not in CFPs).
- No clear evidence was found of a CVPR/WACV/ICCV workshop specifically framed around "social good" classification of advertising content (as opposed to synthetic media forensics); this framing is more consistently found at ICWSM/AAAI/health-informatics venues (see next question).

## Are there venues specifically friendly to "AI for social good," "AI for public health," or "computational social science" framings (AAAI special track, ICTD, AI+HCI, health informatics journals)?

### Takeaway
Yes — AAAI's Special Track on AI for Social Impact, ICWSM, ICTD, and health-informatics journals (JMIR family, Tobacco Control, Drug and Alcohol Review) all explicitly welcome this kind of problem-driven, public-health-framed ML work, and several use review rubrics that reward real-world significance over pure technical novelty — a better fit for a first dataset+classifier paper than a pure-ML venue.

### Cited Findings
- AAAI's Special Track on AI for Social Impact (AISI) reviews submissions on a rubric emphasizing "fit between the techniques used and a problem of social importance" rather than rewarding technical novelty alone; reviewers assess problem significance, engagement with prior application-domain literature, novelty/justification of the approach, evaluation quality, and "overall scope and promise for social impact" — [AAAI-26 Call for the Special Track on AI for Social Impact](https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-26/aisi-call/)
- AAAI-26's AISI track explicitly calls out "data collection challenges," including innovative collection methods, validation techniques, and bias/fairness mitigation strategies, as a key submission area — [AAAI-26 AISI Call](https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-26/aisi-call/)
- AAAI-26 took place Jan 20–27, 2026 in Singapore, giving a sense of the annual cadence (submissions typically ~6-8 months prior, i.e., roughly August of the preceding year, though this must be reverified per cycle) — [AAAI-26 Know Before You Go](https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-26/aaai-26-know-before-you-go/)
- ICWSM is described as "a venue for researchers in the computational social sciences who are interested in exploring social phenomena with algorithmic methods and new kinds of data" — directly matching an "ads as a social/health phenomenon" framing — [ICWSM 2026 Submit](https://www.icwsm.org/2026/submit.html)
- The ICT4D Conference (merged with TechChange for 2026) frames itself around "Data" (opportunities/pitfalls of AI in development work, power dynamics in data collection) and includes "Digital Health" as one of its four key technical themes — directly relevant to an India-based health-policy ML project — [iCT4D Conference 2026](https://www.ict4dconference.org/); [2026 Program](https://www.ict4dconference.org/2026-program)
- JMIR's flagship journal increasingly routes pure ML papers to sister journals (JMIR Medical Informatics, JMIR Formative Research, JMIR AI) unless the ML has reached clinical maturity with direct clinical impact and independent-dataset validation — [JMIR Submission Guide (via Manusights analysis)](https://manusights.com/blog/journal-of-medical-internet-research-submission-guide)
- JMIR's flagship targets an average 2-month decision time for externally peer-reviewed papers, with a fast-track option guaranteeing a decision within 20 business days and publication within 4 weeks of acceptance — [JMIR Review Time](https://manusights.com/blog/journal-of-medical-internet-research-review-time)
- Tobacco Control (BMJ journal) has already published at least one scoping review specifically on ML applications in tobacco research ("Machine learning applications in tobacco research: a scoping review," Jan 2023), confirming the journal's receptiveness to ML-in-tobacco-surveillance framings — [Machine learning applications in tobacco research scoping review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34452986/?dopt=Abstract)
- Drug and Alcohol Review (Wiley) published "Automated Detection of Digital Alcohol Marketing Using SCANNER: An Integrated Deep-Learning Approach" (2026), a close precedent combining deep learning (YOLOv8 + OCR) with alcohol-marketing detection for public-health surveillance — [SCANNER Alcohol paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dar.70095); [PMC version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12741508/)

### Inferences
- A paper framed primarily as a *public-health surveillance tool* (ad detection → policy enforcement) rather than a *novel ML architecture* fits AAAI AISI, ICWSM, ICTD, Tobacco Control, or Drug and Alcohol Review better than NeurIPS/ACM MM, where technical novelty is weighted more heavily.
- Given the project targets Indian ads specifically, ICTD's "Digital Health" theme and its explicit interest in "power dynamics in data collection" in development contexts could make it one of the most natural fits, alongside ICWSM.
- A dual-track strategy is plausible: publish the dataset+baseline model as a CS/ML paper (ICWSM, LREC-COLING, or ARR/EMNLP Findings) and later/separately publish a health-policy-framed analysis paper (Tobacco Control, Drug and Alcohol Review, or JMIR Infodemiology/Formative Research) using the same dataset — mirroring how the SCANNER Alcohol and JMIR tobacco POS surveillance papers are framed as public-health tools, not ML novelty papers.

### Gaps
- Could not verify current JMIR Infodemiology or Digital Health (SAGE) journal-specific submission guidelines or review timelines in this research pass; only the flagship JMIR journal's policy was found directly.
- No AI+HCI venue (e.g., CHI, CSCW) was directly searched in this pass; these are plausible additional venues for the human-annotation/UX-of-moderation angle but were not verified here — flagged as a gap for the report writer to note as an open venue option.

## What is the typical paper structure for a dataset-and-model paper (sections, datasheets, ethics, limitations, reproducibility)?

### Takeaway
Reviewers across NeurIPS, ACL-family venues, and ICWSM now expect a fairly standardized structure: motivation/problem framing, detailed data collection & annotation methodology (ideally formatted per "Datasheets for Datasets"), baseline model + evaluation, a dedicated Limitations section, an Ethics/Broader-Impact statement, and a reproducibility checklist/appendix — and incomplete versions of these can cause desk rejection at some venues.

### Cited Findings
- "Datasheets for Datasets" (Gebru et al.) structures dataset documentation into seven sections: Motivation, Composition, Collection Process, Preprocessing/Cleaning/Labeling, Uses, Distribution, and Maintenance — [Datasheets for Datasets (arXiv:1803.09010)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.09010)
- The Collection Process section of a datasheet should cover consent, notification, and ethical review of the data collection — [Datasheets for Datasets (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.09010)
- The Uses section should document a (potential) repository of papers/systems using the dataset and explicitly flag tasks the dataset should *not* be used for — [Datasheets for Datasets (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.09010)
- NeurIPS requires a submission Paper Checklist; papers that do not include it are desk-rejected — [NeurIPS Paper Checklist Guidelines](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- NeurIPS checklist explicitly asks whether the paper "fully discloses all the information needed to reproduce the main experimental results... regardless of whether code and data are provided," and accepts alternate reproducibility routes (detailed instructions, hosted model access, checkpoints) when full code/data release isn't possible — [NeurIPS Paper Checklist Guidelines](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- NeurIPS authors are "encouraged to create a separate 'Limitations' section" discussing strong assumptions and robustness of results to violations of those assumptions — [NeurIPS Paper Checklist Guidelines](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)
- NeurIPS required a mandatory "Broader Impact" statement in 2020 but replaced this with the current checklist-based approach (asking authors whether they considered negative societal impacts) starting 2021, after review found papers were sometimes rejected or conditionally accepted over broader-impact/ethics concerns — [AI Ethics Statements analysis of NeurIPS Broader Impact Statements](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01705); [NeurIPS 2020 broader impacts experiment discussion](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2020/12/21/the-neurips-2020-broader-impacts-experiment/)
- The ACL Responsible NLP Research checklist asks authors to report: full text of annotator instructions (with screenshots/risk disclaimers), recruitment and payment details (with discussion of payment adequacy relative to annotator demographics), how consent was obtained from people whose data is used/curated, and basic demographic/geographic characteristics of the annotator population — [ACL Rolling Review Responsible NLP Research](https://aclrollingreview.org/responsibleNLPresearch/)
- ACL's ethics guidance states that human-subjects data collection should ideally be approved (or determined exempt) by an ethics review board (e.g., IRB in the US), and this approval status should be stated explicitly in the paper — [ACL Ethical Reviewing Recommendations](https://ethics.aclweb.org/resources/ethical-review-recommendations/)

### Inferences
- For this project, the paper should include (at minimum): (1) Intro/motivation tied to the Indian surrogate-advertising policy problem, (2) Related Work (surrogate advertising regulation + prior ad/tobacco/alcohol detection ML work), (3) Dataset section structured per Datasheets-for-Datasets headings, including an explicit annotator-demographics and consent/IRB-or-exemption statement, (4) Model/Method, (5) Experiments/Baselines, (6) Limitations (separate section), (7) Ethics/Broader Impact statement, (8) Reproducibility checklist/appendix (even if full data can't be released due to scraped-content copyright, the authors should describe an alternative access path, e.g., providing annotation labels + URLs/hashes rather than raw ad images).
- Because the dataset involves scraped ad content and human annotation of a sensitive topic (addiction products), the "Uses" and "Collection Process" datasheet sections are likely to draw the most reviewer scrutiny — the project should proactively document annotator training, consent, and content-sensitivity safeguards (e.g., annotator wellbeing for disturbing content) even though this wasn't found as an explicit named requirement for this specific content type.

### Gaps
- Could not find a venue-specific template or examples of a datasheet applied to an *advertising/marketing content* dataset (most templates examples found were for generic NLP/vision corpora) — the report writer may want to flag this as something to model closely on the PHAD/DEFEND/FLAASH papers' own data sections (see precedents section) rather than a published social-media-ads-specific template.

## What are common review timelines (submission to decision to camera-ready to indexed publication) for a journal vs. a conference/workshop route?

### Takeaway
Conference/workshop routes (NeurIPS, ACM MM, LREC-COLING, ICWSM) generally run on fixed annual/biennial cycles with 2–4 months from submission to notification and a short camera-ready window, while journals (JMIR family) can be faster per-cycle (an average ~2-month first decision, with a 20-business-day fast-track option) but have no fixed "issue" cadence and often require revision rounds that extend total time; exact comparative totals (e.g., submission to final indexed publication) were not fully quantified in available sources for every venue and are noted as a gap below.

### Cited Findings
- LREC 2026: paper deadline 17 Oct 2025 → notification of acceptance 13 Feb 2026 (≈4 months) → camera-ready 15 Mar 2026 (≈1 month after notification) → conference (publication/indexing typically follows shortly after/at the conference) 11–16 May 2026 — [LREC 2026 Calls](https://lrec2026.info/calls/)
- ACM MM 2026: abstract 25 Mar 2026 → full paper 1 Apr 2026 → supplementary 8 Apr 2026 → notification 7 Jul 2026 (≈3 months from full paper deadline) → conference 10–14 Nov 2026 (camera-ready window falls between notification and conference, exact date not found) — [ACM MM 2026 CFP Guidelines](https://2026.acmmm.org/site/cfp-guidelines.html)
- NeurIPS 2026 E&D track: abstract 4 May 2026 → full paper 6 May 2026 → (notification timing for E&D not separately specified beyond "same as main track," which for NeurIPS historically runs ~4-5 months to notification, with the conference itself in Dec) — [NeurIPS 2026 E&D CFP](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForEvaluationsDatasets)
- ICWSM-2025 dataset track: deadline 15 Jan 2025 → notification 15 Mar 2025 (exactly 2 months) — [ICWSM dataset submission page](https://www.icwsm.org/2025/submit/index.html)
- JMIR flagship journal: average ~2-month first decision after being sent for peer review; fast-track option guarantees decision within 20 business days (~4 weeks) and publication within 4 weeks of acceptance — [JMIR Review Time analysis](https://manusights.com/blog/journal-of-medical-internet-research-review-time)
- ACL Rolling Review (ARR) operates on a continuous-cycle model (papers submitted in "regular intervals called cycles," receiving reviews + meta-review), which decouples review timing from any single conference's deadline, but a paper must still be "committed" to a specific venue (ACL/EMNLP/NAACL/Findings) after review to be published — [ACL Rolling Review Overview](https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp)

### Inferences
- A conference/workshop route (LREC, ICWSM, ACM MM dataset track) likely offers a more predictable total timeline (submission → notification in 2–4 months, camera-ready shortly after, publication/indexing essentially at the conference date) than a traditional journal route, where revision rounds are common and "indexed publication" can lag acceptance by additional weeks to months depending on the journal's production queue.
- ARR's continuous-cycle, decoupled-from-conference-deadline model gives more flexibility for resubmission without starting a brand-new review from scratch, which could be valuable if first-round reviews require substantial revision — relevant for a first-time team's risk management.
- If the core motivation is rapid dissemination into policy circles, the JMIR fast-track (as fast as ~8 weeks submission-to-publication) is the quickest path found in this research, faster than any conference cycle identified.

### Gaps
- No source directly quantified "notification to camera-ready to indexed-in-proceedings" time for NeurIPS, nor the typical time from JMIR acceptance to indexing in PubMed/PMC (as opposed to publication on the JMIR site) — flagged as unresolved.
- No data found on Tobacco Control's or Drug and Alcohol Review's own review-to-publication timelines specifically (only that they have published ML-adjacent work).

## What ethical/IRB/data-rights considerations are typically expected for scraped social media/ad content + human annotation, and what do top venues require?

### Takeaway
Top NLP/ML venues (ACL/ARR, NeurIPS) do not uniformly require formal IRB approval for scraped/public data or annotation work, but they do require authors to proactively disclose ethical review status (or explain why none was sought/needed), document consent and annotator-protection practices, and flag broader societal impacts/misuse risks — with a specific ethics-review escalation path (ethics advisers/flagging) for submissions that raise concern.

### Cited Findings
- ACL's ethics guidance states that "ethics review (e.g., from an IRB board in the US context) may be required for any human subjects research," and that if an ethics board was involved, authors should clearly state so in the paper — but also clarifies that "the involvement of human annotators in a study does not on its own justify an ethics review" unless there are specific concerns (e.g., potential exploitation or ethical lapses) — [ACL Ethical Reviewing Recommendations](https://ethics.aclweb.org/resources/ethical-review-recommendations/)
- ACL's Responsible NLP checklist requires disclosure of: annotator instructions/screenshots/risk disclaimers; recruitment and payment details with a judgment on payment adequacy relative to demographics; how consent was obtained from people whose data is used; and basic annotator demographic/geographic characteristics — [ACL Rolling Review Responsible NLP Research](https://aclrollingreview.org/responsibleNLPresearch/)
- ACL Rolling Review has a formal "Ethics Flagging" process (ARR Ethics Review Flagging Guidelines) through which reviewers/ACs can escalate submissions for a dedicated ethics review — [ACL ARR Ethics Review Flagging Guidelines](https://aclrollingreview.org/ethics-flagging-guidelines/)
- NeurIPS's ethics process (in the 2020 Broader Impact experiment) allowed reviewers to flag submissions for ethical concerns; flagged papers with otherwise strong technical reviews were routed to a pool of ethics advisers, and some were rejected or conditionally accepted pending revision of the broader-impact section or removal of problematic datasets — [AI Ethics Statements analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01705)
- NeurIPS's current (post-2021) approach replaced the mandatory Broader Impact section with a checklist item asking whether authors "considered potential negative societal impacts" of their work — [AI Ethics Statements analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01705)
- Datasheets for Datasets explicitly recommends documenting consent/notification/ethical-review status as part of the "Collection Process" datasheet section, and flagging whether the dataset contains confidential or sensitive content as part of "Composition" — [Datasheets for Datasets (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.09010)

### Inferences
- For this project (scraping Indian ads, which may include copyrighted creative content and brand material, plus human annotation of promotional-vs-preventive intent), the paper should proactively include: (a) a statement on IRB status for the human-annotation component (even if "determined exempt" because annotators are labeling public ad content rather than being human subjects themselves), (b) a clear fair-use/research-use justification for scraping copyrighted ad creative, (c) annotator demographic disclosure and payment-adequacy discussion per the ACL checklist, and (d) an explicit dataset release plan addressing copyright (e.g., releasing annotations + URLs/hashes rather than redistributing raw ad images, similar to how many social-media content datasets are released).
- Because the content concerns addiction-related products, the annotator-wellbeing dimension (exposure to promotional content for addictive substances) is a plausible reviewer concern that isn't explicitly named in any source found but logically follows from the general "annotator protection" expectations in the ACL checklist — the authors should consider explicitly addressing it even absent a named rule.

### Gaps
- No source directly addressed IRB/ethics expectations specific to *advertising content* scraping (as opposed to generic social media text/image scraping) — inferred by analogy above rather than confirmed.
- Could not find ICWSM- or ACM-MM-specific ethics-review processes in this pass (only general ACL/ARR and NeurIPS policies were verified); the report writer should treat ICWSM/ACM MM ethics requirements as an open item if venue-specific detail is needed.
- Indian-specific data-protection law considerations (e.g., India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act) relevant to scraping Indian ad content and annotator data were not researched in this pass and are a notable gap given the project's India focus.

## Are there precedents of papers on surrogate advertising detection, tobacco/alcohol ad classification, or similar health-policy ML papers, and where were they published?

### Takeaway
There is no found precedent using the specific legal term "surrogate advertising" in an ML/NLP paper, but there is a small and growing cluster of very closely analogous multimodal tobacco/alcohol-content classification papers — published across arXiv preprints, IJCV (under review), EMNLP workshops, JMIR, and Drug and Alcohol Review — giving concrete venue precedent for this project's exact problem shape (new dataset + multimodal baseline classifier for addiction-related content).

### Cited Findings
- A search specifically for "surrogate advertising" + India + alcohol/tobacco + dataset/NLP returned only non-ML sources (marketing/legal/regulatory studies on surrogate advertising's prevalence and effectiveness in India, e.g., in cricket broadcasts), with no evidence found of an existing ML/NLP paper using this specific framing — [Surrogate advertising (Wikipedia overview)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrogate_advertising); [Cricket, commerce, and public health: promotion of tobacco, alcohol, and HFSS products](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12241050/); [Hidden in Plain Sight: Surrogate Marketing of Tobacco (Vital Strategies report)](http://www.vitalstrategies.org/wp-content/uploads/TERM_HiddeninPlainSight_December-2022.pdf)
- "Public Health Advocacy Dataset (PHAD)" (arXiv:2411.13572) is a close precedent: 5,730 tobacco-related videos from TikTok/YouTube (4.3M frames) with a two-stage classification approach using a Vision-Language encoder; the paper is stated to be under review at the International Journal of Computer Vision (IJCV) — [PHAD arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13572); [TechRxiv preprint](https://www.techrxiv.org/doi/full/10.36227/techrxiv.172565540.01845016/v3)
- "DEFEND: A Large-scale 1M Dataset and Foundation Model for Tobacco Addiction Prevention" (arXiv:2501.13950) introduces "Tobacco-1M," one million tobacco product images with hierarchical labels across 75 categories, plus a foundation model; found as an arXiv/TechRxiv preprint and associated with a University of Arkansas student research repository, suggesting it may still be in the pre-peer-review or thesis stage rather than published at a flagship venue — [DEFEND arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13950); [University of Arkansas ScholarWorks listing](https://scholarworks.uark.edu/elcsuht/16/)
- "FLAASH: Flow-Attention Adaptive Semantic Hierarchical Fusion for Multi-Modal Tobacco Content Analysis" evaluates on a "Multimodal Tobacco Content Analysis Dataset (MTCAD)" of tobacco-related videos; found via arXiv/ResearchGate, specific peer-reviewed venue not confirmed in this pass — [FLAASH arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.19896); [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383174257_FLAASH_Flow-Attention_Adaptive_Semantic_Hierarchical_Fusion_for_Multi-Modal_Tobacco_Content_Analysis)
- "SmokEng: Towards Fine-grained Classification of Tobacco-related Social Media Text" was published at the W-NUT workshop co-located with EMNLP 2019 (ACL Anthology ID D19-5524), using a dataset of 3,144 tweets selected for colloquial smoking-related slang — a direct precedent for a workshop-level NLP dataset+classifier paper on addiction-related content — [SmokEng ACL Anthology page](https://aclanthology.org/D19-5524/); [SmokEng arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05598)
- "Image Processing for Public Health Surveillance of Tobacco Point-of-Sale Advertising: Machine Learning–Based Methodology" was published in the Journal of Medical Internet Research (JMIR) in 2021, using Inception V3 for logo classification and YOLOv3 for logo localization on photographs of tobacco point-of-sale advertising, achieving >75% classification accuracy for 8 of 42 brands tested — a strong precedent for a *health-policy-journal* framing of ad-surveillance ML work — [JMIR paper](https://www.jmir.org/2021/8/e24408/PDF); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/24408)
- "Automated Detection of Digital Alcohol Marketing Using SCANNER: An Integrated Deep-Learning Approach" was published in Drug and Alcohol Review (Wiley) in 2026, combining YOLOv8 (logo/object detection) and PaddleOCR (text) to identify 134 alcohol brands in image/video data, reporting mAP 0.94, recall 0.96, F1 0.95, and 98.9% correct identification of alcohol-branded social media posts in real-world testing — the most directly analogous published precedent found (multimodal, deep-learning, alcohol marketing, public-health-journal venue) — [SCANNER Alcohol, Drug and Alcohol Review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dar.70095); [PMC version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12741508/)
- "Evaluating AI models for food and alcohol advertisement classification against human benchmarks" (PMC) is another direct precedent explicitly benchmarking AI ad-classification models against human raters — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13099998/)
- "Machine learning applications in tobacco research: a scoping review" was published in Tobacco Control (Jan 2023), confirming the journal actively covers ML-in-tobacco-surveillance work as a topic area (via scoping review, not just original research) — [PubMed listing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34452986/?dopt=Abstract)
- "Harnessing machine learning in contemporary tobacco research" and "Artificial intelligence and tobacco use: A bibliometric analysis 1997–2026" (both PMC) further confirm an active, citable literature base at the intersection of ML and tobacco-control research — [Harnessing ML in tobacco research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11750557/); [AI and tobacco use bibliometric analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13524066/)

### Inferences
- The project sits in a real, small-but-growing niche: "multimodal addiction-product-content classification" has precedent at the preprint/workshop level (PHAD, DEFEND, FLAASH — all arXiv, mostly unconfirmed peer-reviewed venues yet) and at the published-journal level specifically for *advertising/marketing detection* (JMIR tobacco POS paper, Drug and Alcohol Review SCANNER paper). This suggests two realistic, precedented routes: (1) an ML/CS-venue dataset paper (style of PHAD/DEFEND/SmokEng, i.e., arXiv + a CS workshop/conference such as EMNLP Findings/W-NUT-style workshop, ICWSM, or LREC-COLING), or (2) a health-policy-journal paper modeled directly on the JMIR and Drug and Alcohol Review precedents, which both frame the deep-learning model as a public-health surveillance tool rather than a novel architecture contribution.
- No paper was found using the "surrogate advertising" framing specifically, which is a potential novelty/gap the project could claim — being apparently the first ML paper to operationalize India's surrogate-advertising legal category (promotional disguised as another product) as a classification target, as distinct from the more generic "is this an alcohol/tobacco ad" framing used by SCANNER and the JMIR POS paper.
