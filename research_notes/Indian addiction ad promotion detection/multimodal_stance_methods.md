# Multimodal ML Methods for Stance/Intent/Persuasion/Sentiment Classification in Advertisements (for Indian addiction-ad promoting-vs-preventing task)

## What is the "Automatic Understanding of Image and Video Advertisements" line of work (Hussain et al. / Pitt Ads dataset) and what persuasion-strategy / sentiment / topic classification methods did it use?

### Takeaway
The Pitt Ads dataset (Hussain, Zhang, Kovashka et al., CVPR 2017) is the foundational benchmark for computational ad-understanding, with annotations for topic, sentiment, persuasion strategy, and the implicit "action/reason" the ad wants the viewer to take; follow-on work (AAAI 2023 "Persuasion Strategies in Advertisements") built dedicated multi-task, multi-modal attention-fusion models for persuasion-strategy prediction, and a 2024 ACL paper shows modern LLMs/VLMs still underperform on this task, meaning there is no "solved" off-the-shelf model to borrow directly — architecture choices still matter.

### Cited Findings
- The Pitt Ads dataset contains 64,832 image ads and 3,477 video ads; ads are both product ads and advocacy/PSA-style ads (e.g., for environmental preservation, against bullying) — structurally analogous to promoting-vs-preventing framing. — [Automatic Understanding of Image and Video Advertisements, CVPR 2017 (PDF)](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/hussain_zhang_kovashka_ads_cvpr2017.pdf); [dataset page](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/ads/)
- Image ads are annotated with topic (38 categories), sentiment (30 categories, e.g. "cheerful," "disturbed," "sad"), question/answer action-reason statements ("What should the viewer do according to this ad?" / "Why?"), symbolism, strategy, and slogan; video ads are annotated with topic, sentiment, action/reason, funniness, excitement, language, and effectiveness. — [Pitt Ads dataset page](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/ads/); [HuggingFace dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/creative-graphic-design/PittImageVideoAdsDataset)
- Reported baselines on Pitt Ads: 152-layer ResNet topic classification achieved 60.34% accuracy (38-way); the sentiment classification network achieved only 27.92% accuracy (30-way), showing sentiment/affect is markedly harder than topic/object recognition in ads. — [CVPR 2017 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Hussain_Automatic_Understanding_of_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)
- Follow-on work, "Persuasion Strategies in Advertisements" (Kumar, Dangi, Kovashka et al., AAAI 2023), created the first ad-image corpus annotated with fine-grained persuasion strategies (with image segmentation masks localizing which region triggers which strategy) and a real-world case study of 1,600 ad campaigns from 30 Fortune-500 companies. — [arXiv:2208.09626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09626); [AAAI proceedings version](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/25076/24848)
- That paper's model is a multi-task attention-fusion architecture that jointly leverages other ad-understanding tasks (e.g., topic, sentiment, symbolism prediction) as auxiliary signals to predict persuasion strategy, combining text, image, and symbolism modalities, and uses active learning to reduce the cost of labeling fine-grained persuasion-strategy data. — [arXiv:2208.09626 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09626)
- A separately-reported result for persuasion-strategy prediction on this line of work: top-1 and top-3 accuracy of 59.2% and 84.8% respectively when trained with 2,500 labeled samples. — [search-aggregated result citing the Pitt Ads/persuasion-strategy literature](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/25076/24848) (exact figure not independently re-verified from the PDF body text; treat as moderately confident)
- A 2024 ACL short paper, "Don't Buy It! Reassessing the Ad Understanding Abilities," specifically re-evaluates modern LLMs and VLMs (including CLIP-based models) on Pitt-Ads-style persuasion-strategy classification and finds these general-purpose multimodal models still struggle to recognize rhetorical/persuasion techniques, fail to meaningfully integrate visual and textual persuasive cues, and generalize poorly to novel advertising approaches — i.e., current foundation VLMs are not a plug-and-play solution for this task. — [ACL Anthology 2024.acl-short.77](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-short.77.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because sentiment/affect classification lags far behind topic classification on the same dataset and architecture family (ResNet), a promoting-vs-preventing classifier (which is closer to a "sentiment/stance" task than a "topic" task) should expect topic-classification-level accuracy to be an unrealistic ceiling; benchmarks in the 55–75% range for a binary or few-way affect/stance task are more realistic starting expectations, consistent with multi-task/attention-fusion gains reported in the persuasion-strategy literature.
- The explicit design precedent of multi-task learning (jointly predicting topic + sentiment + symbolism + persuasion strategy) and attention-based fusion over heterogeneous ad annotations is directly transferable to an Indian-addiction-ad task: a promoting/preventing label could be one head of a multi-task model trained jointly with auxiliary heads (e.g., disclaimer presence, product category, tone).
- Given the ACL 2024 finding that off-the-shelf VLMs/LLMs underperform on persuasion-strategy classification, a project with a limited GPU budget may get more value from fine-tuning a smaller task-specific fusion model (as Hussain et al. and the AAAI 2023 paper did) than from zero-/few-shot prompting a large VLM, though VLMs could still serve as a quick baseline.

- Could not independently verify the exact persuasion-strategy classification F1/accuracy numbers from the full AAAI 2023 paper body (PDF text extraction failed); only abstract-level methodology was retrievable.
- No benchmark numbers found specifically for video-ad persuasion/sentiment classification (as opposed to image ads) in the Pitt Ads line of work — the video subset (3,477 ads) appears used mainly for effectiveness/funniness/excitement labels rather than persuasion-strategy prediction.

## What are the leading approaches for video-text-audio multimodal fusion for classification tasks (late fusion, cross-attention transformers, CLIP/CLIP4Clip-style joint embeddings, VideoMAE, TimeSformer, Video-LLaVA, VideoCLIP)?

### Takeaway
The field has moved from early/late fusion of independently-pretrained unimodal encoders toward transformer-based cross-modal attention and "fusion bottleneck" architectures that mix modalities at multiple internal layers; for the video encoder specifically, there are two practical open-source families — CLIP-based joint embeddings (CLIP4Clip, CLIP-ViP) for retrieval/alignment-style tasks, and self-supervised masked video transformers (VideoMAE, TimeSformer) for classification — plus large instruction-tuned video-LLMs (Video-LLaVA, VideoCLIP) that unify video and language representations for zero-/few-shot or generative use.

### Cited Findings
- **Cross-attention/cascaded transformers**: CCMT (Cascaded Cross-Modal Transformer) converts an audio-visual-text classification problem into an audio-textual classification task via cascaded cross-attention transformer blocks that iteratively combine linguistic and audio features. — [Cascaded cross-modal transformer for audio–textual classification, Artificial Intelligence Review 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-024-10869-1)
- **Multimodal Bottleneck Transformer (MBT)**: rather than traditional late fusion, MBT restricts cross-modal information flow through a small number of "fusion bottleneck" latent tokens, forcing each modality to condense only the most relevant information before sharing it with other modalities; fusion happens across multiple transformer layers (not just at the end). MBT achieved state-of-the-art results on AudioSet, Epic-Kitchens, and VGGSound audio-visual benchmarks, and the authors released all code and models. Published at NeurIPS 2021. — [Attention Bottlenecks for Multimodal Fusion, arXiv:2107.00135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00135)
- **Emotion recognition cross-attention**: AVT-CA (Audio-Video Transformer with Cross Attention) synchronizes interlinked audio and video features and selectively emphasizes critical features while discarding irrelevant ones per modality — directly relevant as a template for an audio+video ad-classification head. — [Multimodal Emotion Recognition using Audio-Video Transformer, arXiv:2407.18552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.18552)
- **MemoCMT**: a cross-modal transformer-based feature-fusion architecture for multimodal emotion recognition, published in Scientific Reports 2025, offering a recent, documented cross-modal transformer fusion recipe. — [MemoCMT, Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-89202-x)
- **CLIP4Clip**: transfers CLIP's joint image-text embedding knowledge to video-language retrieval end-to-end, with three components — a video encoder, a text encoder, and a similarity calculator — achieving state-of-the-art results on MSR-VTT, MSVD, LSMDC, ActivityNet, and DiDeMo retrieval benchmarks. — [arXiv:2104.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08860)
- **CLIP-ViP**: adapts pretrained image-text CLIP models to video-language representation alignment, part of the same "CLIP-based framework" family as CLIP4Clip and CLIP2Video. — [arXiv:2209.06430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.06430)
- **TimeSformer**: an "optimized temporal transformer" used as the visual encoder inside video-language models such as FiT, applying attention across both space and time dimensions of video frames. — [search-aggregated from ViLMA/FiT literature](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07022)
- **VideoMAE**: a self-supervised video masked-autoencoder using an asymmetric encoder-decoder and a "tube masking" strategy (masking the same spatial locations across all frames in a segment) at very high masking ratios (90–95%). It is notably data-efficient — can be trained with as few as 3.5k videos — and reports 87.4% top-1 on Kinetics-400, 75.4% on Something-Something V2, 91.3% on UCF101, and 62.6% on HMDB51 without extra pretraining data. VideoMAE V2 scales this with dual masking. Open-source, available via HuggingFace Transformers. — [arXiv:2203.12602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12602); [VideoMAE V2, arXiv:2303.16727](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16727); [HuggingFace docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/videomae)
- **VideoCLIP**: a contrastive pretraining approach for zero-shot video-and-text understanding; trains a transformer by contrasting temporally-overlapping positive video-text pairs against hard negatives mined via nearest-neighbor retrieval, without using any labels for the downstream task. Demonstrates strong performance on text-video retrieval, VideoQA, action localization, and action segmentation. — [ACL Anthology 2021.emnlp-main.544](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.544.pdf)
- **Video-LLaVA**: unifies image and video representations into a shared language-aligned feature space (via a shared visual encoder, e.g., LanguageBind or OpenCLIP ViT-L/14) before feeding an LLM, enabling video understanding, complex visual reasoning, and natural-language generation. Training uses two stages: "understanding pretraining" (558k image-text pairs + 702k video-text pairs) and "instruction tuning" (665k image-text + 100k video-text instruction data). — [ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-main.342](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.342.pdf)
- **Fusion-method survey**: multimodal sentiment analysis fusion techniques span early, late, hybrid, tensor, contextual, hierarchical, graph, and attention-mechanism-based fusion; one benchmark reported early fusion significantly outperforming late fusion (71.87% vs. lower late-fusion accuracy), with multi-head attention adding only marginal further improvement (72.39%), while a transformer-based early-fusion model reported 97.87% 7-class accuracy / 0.9682 F1 on a specific test set (dataset/task not fully specified in the retrieved snippet). — [Multimodal Sentiment Analysis on CMU-MOSEI Dataset using Transformer-based Models, arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06110v1)
- **CMU-MOSEI** benchmark: 3,228 real-world videos, 23,453 utterances, >1,000 speakers, 250 topics, ~65 hours total, combining text, video, and audio — the most widely used multimodal sentiment/emotion benchmark and a useful sanity-check dataset for fusion-architecture prototyping before committing to the ad-specific dataset. — [search-aggregated survey result](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390221365_Multimodal_Sentiment_Analysis-A_Comprehensive_Survey_From_a_Fusion_Methods_Perspective)

### Inferences
- For a limited-GPU-budget project, the most practical stack is: (1) VideoMAE (or a pretrained CLIP-ViT image encoder sampled at low fps) as a frozen or lightly-fine-tuned visual feature extractor, (2) a pretrained audio model (e.g., Whisper encoder or wav2vec2) or ASR transcript + text transformer for the audio/speech channel, and (3) an MBT-style bottleneck or cross-attention fusion layer on top — all three components have open-source code and pretrained weights, avoiding full multimodal pretraining from scratch.
- VideoCLIP/CLIP4Clip-style contrastive video-text alignment is best suited to retrieval-style tasks; for a direct classification task like promoting-vs-preventing, a supervised fusion+classification head (MBT/cross-attention) fine-tuned on labeled ad data is likely to be more sample-efficient than relying on zero-shot CLIP-style similarity scoring, especially given the ACL 2024 finding that VLMs underperform on ad-persuasion understanding.
- Video-LLaVA / video-LLMs are attractive for an interpretable, few-shot baseline (e.g., prompting "does this ad promote or discourage substance use?") but likely require substantial GPU memory (7B+ parameter LLM backbones) that may exceed a limited compute budget without quantization.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve a primary TimeSformer paper directly (CVPR/ICCV original) — the finding above is inferred from secondary citations (ViLMA/FiT papers) rather than the original TimeSformer paper itself.
- Did not find head-to-head accuracy comparisons between MBT, VideoMAE+fusion, and VideoCLIP on the *same* downstream classification benchmark; cross-architecture comparison numbers are sparse because each paper targets different benchmarks (AudioSet/Epic-Kitchens for MBT vs. Kinetics/SSv2 for VideoMAE vs. retrieval benchmarks for CLIP4Clip/VideoCLIP).
- Specific compute/GPU requirements (VRAM, training hours) for fine-tuning these models were not directly discoverable in the retrieved sources; general knowledge suggests VideoMAE-base and MBT are feasible on a single consumer/prosumer GPU (e.g., 16–24GB) for fine-tuning with frozen backbones, while Video-LLaVA-class models typically need multi-GPU or quantized inference, but this specific claim was not found in a cited source and should be treated as an inference, not a verified fact.

## What methods exist for stance detection and persuasion-technique detection in NLP (SemEval stance tasks, SemEval-2020 Task 11 propaganda detection, argument mining)?

### Takeaway
Stance detection in NLP has progressed from early RNN/Word2Vec target-conditioned encoders (SemEval-2016 Task 6) to transformer-based target-aware models and now LLM-based few-shot classification; propaganda/persuasion-technique detection (SemEval-2020 Task 11) frames the problem as span identification + multi-label technique classification over a fixed taxonomy of 14 techniques, with BERT/RoBERTa-based ensembles dominating the leaderboard — both task framings are directly adaptable to localizing and classifying "promoting" vs. "preventing" rhetorical cues within ad transcripts.

### Cited Findings
- Stance detection is defined as determining whether a text is "in favor," "against," or "none/neutral" toward a target issue. — [Stance Detection: A Survey, ACM 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3369026)
- SemEval-2016 Task 6 was the earliest major stance-detection shared task: 2,900 tweets labeled across 5 targets (Atheism, Climate Change, Feminist Movement, Hillary Clinton, Legalization of Abortion) with 3 stance labels (favor/against/none). — [SemEval-2016 Task 6, ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/S16-1003.pdf)
- The winning SemEval-2016 system (Zarrella and Marsh) used transfer learning with a four-layer RNN, Word2Vec embeddings, and a hashtag-prediction auxiliary task, achieving the top macro-average F-score of 67.8%; 19 teams submitted machine/deep learning models, with the top two being deep-learning-based. — [Stance Detection surveys, aggregated](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.03644)
- Common stance-detection architecture pattern: LSTM "conditional encoding" that represents the (tweet, target) pair jointly, i.e., target-conditioned sequence encoding, rather than encoding the text alone. — [Stance Detection surveys, aggregated](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.03644)
- SemEval-2020 Task 11 ("Detection of Propaganda Techniques in News Articles") had two subtasks: **Span Identification (SI)** — spot the specific text fragments containing propaganda in a plain-text document — and **Technique Classification (TC)** — given a flagged fragment (in full-document context), classify which of 14 propaganda techniques it uses. 250 teams signed up; 44 made test-set submissions. — [SemEval-2020 Task 11, ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.semeval-1.186/); [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/3952415)
- For both SI and TC subtasks, the best-performing systems used pretrained Transformers (BERT/RoBERTa-family) and ensembling. — [SemEval-2020 Task 11 overview, search-aggregated from ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.semeval-1.186/)
- Example participant systems: UPB used domain-specific fine-tuned BERT ("Propaganda Detection with Domain-Specific Trained BERT") — [arXiv:2009.05289](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.05289); JUST used a BERT pretrained model for technique detection — [ACL Anthology 2020.semeval-1.229](https://aclanthology.org/2020.semeval-1.229/); BPGC combined multi-granularity knowledge sharing with linguistic-feature-based ensemble learning — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/BPGC-at-SemEval-2020-Task-11:-Propaganda-Detection-Patil-Singh/106a8610d7bd4840fa1f554818abffd3d05c999c).
- Recent surveys note the field is moving toward deep-learning and now LLM-based stance detection, including cross-target stance detection (generalizing a stance model to unseen targets) as an active research challenge. — [Deep Learning in Stance Detection: A Survey, ACM 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3744641); [Cross-target stance detection survey, ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417425014125)

### Inferences
- The SemEval-2020 Task 11 "span identification + technique classification" framing is a strong template for the disclaimer-vs-main-message problem: one could first localize ad-transcript spans carrying the "real" promotional/preventive message (span identification) and then classify the technique/stance of each span (technique classification), rather than classifying the whole ad transcript as one bag of text — this naturally down-weights a brief disclaimer relative to a longer promotional narrative.
- The stance-detection pattern of "target-conditioned encoding" (jointly encoding text and the target/issue, e.g., LSTM conditional encoding, or now cross-attention between text and a target embedding) is directly applicable: the "target" here would be the specific addictive product/behavior, and the model would predict stance (favor/against) toward that specific target within the ad.
- Fine-tuned BERT/RoBERTa-family text encoders remain a strong, low-compute baseline for the text-stance component of the pipeline, consistent with their dominance in both SemEval-2016 and SemEval-2020 leaderboards.

## How have researchers handled "framing"/intent classification (as opposed to topic/object classification) in advertising, political messaging, or health communication content?

### Takeaway
Framing research distinguishes topic (what an ad is about) from frame/intent (how the topic is presented and what action it implies), and the dominant computational approach is supervised ML trained on human-coded frame taxonomies (deductive/top-down coding schemes) applied to political campaign messages and ads, with health communication research specifically studying "action framing" for behavior-change messaging — directly analogous to the promoting-vs-preventing distinction needed for this project.

### Cited Findings
- Framing is defined as a communicator intentionally emphasizing (or ignoring) certain dimensions of an issue; four distinguishable fields of framing research exist: strategic framing by communicators, framing in journalists' minds, frames embedded in media content, and framing effects on audience members. — [Frames in Political Communication, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281727849_Frames_in_Political_Communication_Towards_Clarification_of_a_Research_Program)
- Supervised frame detection typically works by manually coding texts against a pre-existing frame taxonomy, then training ML classifiers on that labeled data — described as more labor-intensive than purely data-driven/unsupervised topic modeling but more theoretically grounded and reliable. — [Categorizing Campaign Messages using Supervised Machine Learning](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/301999/2/301999.pdf)
- This supervised-coding approach has been applied cross-culturally to classify political messages on Facebook and Twitter, and is noted as applicable to advertisements specifically. — [Categorizing Campaign Messages using Supervised Machine Learning](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/301999/2/301999.pdf)
- "Action framing" is specifically identified as central to health communication efforts that promote healthy behaviors — i.e., framing research explicitly studies messages designed to prompt or discourage a behavior, which is structurally the same task as classifying an ad as promoting or preventing addictive-substance use. — [Seven Models of Framing, Hallahan](https://sites.pitt.edu/~mitnick/BPweb/HallahanSevenModelsFramingJPubRelRes.pdf)
- Social-movement framing research on social media decomposes framing into "diagnostic" (what's the problem), "prognostic" (what's the solution), and "motivational" (why act) framing strategies, which are extracted and classified computationally. — [Framing Social Movements on Social Media, arXiv 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13820v1)
- Political-advertisement-specific ML work includes detecting policy-related political ads in Meta's 2022 French election ad archive, and broader analysis of online political advertisements using ML text/metadata classifiers. — [On Detecting Policy-Related Political Ads, arXiv:2302.06917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06917); [Analyzing Online Political Advertisements, arXiv:2105.04047](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.04047)
- Recent work uses LLMs for political-science text classification with automatic prompt optimization and dynamic few-shot exemplar selection, suggesting LLM-based framing/frame-classification is now a viable low-training-data alternative to fully supervised pipelines. — [Enhancing LLM-Based Text Classification in Political Science, arXiv:2409.01466](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.01466)

### Inferences
- The diagnostic/prognostic/motivational framing decomposition is a useful conceptual lens for an addiction ad: a "preventing" ad is likely to foreground diagnostic framing (the problem/harm of the product) and motivational framing (reasons to quit/avoid), while a "promoting" ad foregrounds positive lifestyle/aspirational framing — this suggests a frame-taxonomy-based multi-label annotation layer (beyond a single binary label) could substantially improve model training signal and interpretability.
- Given the Gla.ac.uk and Hallahan sources, the most defensible methodology for this project is to build a small theoretically-grounded frame taxonomy (e.g., health-harm framing, aspirational/lifestyle framing, legal-disclaimer framing, peer-normalization framing) and have it human-coded before training classifiers — mirroring standard political-communication framing research practice rather than treating promoting/preventing as a purely data-driven label.
- LLM-based few-shot/prompt-optimized classification (as in the political-science paper) is a plausible low-cost baseline to pair with a fine-tuned transformer, especially useful for a limited-labeled-data regime typical of a niche Indian-ad dataset.

### Gaps
- Did not find papers applying framing analysis specifically to multimodal (video) advertisements as opposed to text-only political messages; the framing literature retrieved is predominantly text-based (campaign messages, social media posts), so its extension to video/audio ad framing is an inference, not a directly cited finding.
- Did not find framing-classification work specific to South Asian/Indian media content in this search pass — flagged as a notable gap given the project's specific focus on Indian advertisements.

## What are common baseline architectures for ad-understanding tasks and what accuracy/F1 benchmarks are reported?

### Takeaway
Reported baselines for ad-understanding tasks cluster around CNN/ResNet vision backbones for topic/object-level tasks (where accuracy is moderate-to-good, ~60-75%), much lower accuracy for sentiment/affect tasks (~28%) under the same backbone family, and multimodal text+image deep learning approaches reaching higher accuracy (74-98%) on narrower, more object-grounded tasks like tobacco/logo detection — indicating that the more abstract/subjective the target label (sentiment, frame, stance) the harder the task and the more fusion/multi-task architecture matters.

### Cited Findings
- Pitt Ads topic classification (38-way) with a 152-layer ResNet: 60.34% accuracy. — [CVPR 2017 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Hussain_Automatic_Understanding_of_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)
- Pitt Ads sentiment classification (30-way) with the same backbone family: 27.92% accuracy. — [CVPR 2017 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Hussain_Automatic_Understanding_of_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)
- Persuasion-strategy prediction (fine-grained, trained on 2,500 labeled samples): top-1 accuracy 59.2%, top-3 accuracy 84.8%. — [AAAI 2023 persuasion-strategies paper, aggregated](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/25076/24848) (moderate confidence; see Gap above about PDF text extraction)
- Tobacco point-of-sale advertising detection using pretrained Inception V3 (for logo classification) and YOLOv3 (for logo localization) achieved classification accuracy "over 75%" for identifying advertising presence in street-view images. — [Image Processing for Public Health Surveillance of Tobacco Point-of-Sale Advertising, JMIR / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8433867/)
- A multimodal (text+image) deep learning model for smoking-related content detection with a "small data approach" achieved 74% accuracy on images and 98% accuracy on text. — [A multimodal deep learning architecture for smoking detection with a small data approach, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10936563/)
- SCANNER Alcohol (integrated object detection + OCR for alcohol-brand/logo identification in digital marketing) achieved mean average precision 0.94, recall 0.96, F1 0.95 for detecting alcohol marketing content. — [Automated Detection of Digital Alcohol Marketing Using SCANNER, Drug and Alcohol Review 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dar.70095)
- DEFEND (Tobacco-1M foundation model, 1M tobacco product images, 75 hierarchical product categories): 83.1% accuracy on product classification, 73.8% accuracy on visual question-answering, and only 45.6% accuracy on zero-shot classification of novel/emerging tobacco product categories — showing a sharp drop when generalizing beyond the training distribution of product types. — [arXiv:2501.13950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13950)
- "Don't Buy It!" (ACL 2024) finds that modern general-purpose LLMs/VLMs (including CLIP-based models) substantially underperform specialized/fine-tuned models on Pitt-Ads persuasion-strategy classification, with degraded performance especially on unfamiliar advertising approaches. — [ACL Anthology 2024.acl-short.77](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-short.77.pdf)
- CMU-MOSEI multimodal sentiment benchmark: an early-fusion transformer baseline reported 71.87% accuracy, multi-head attention fusion 72.39%, and one transformer-based early-fusion configuration reported 97.87% 7-class accuracy with 0.9682 F1 (likely on a coarser or different label granularity / data split — treat as not directly comparable across these numbers without checking the exact task definition). — [arXiv:2505.06110v1](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06110v1)

### Inferences
- Across these results, tasks closer to concrete object/logo/brand detection (SCANNER Alcohol F1 0.95, tobacco logo detection ~75%+, text-based smoking detection 98%) report much higher accuracy than tasks closer to subjective sentiment/persuasion/framing judgment (Pitt Ads sentiment 27.92%, persuasion strategy 59.2% top-1). A promoting-vs-preventing classification task is squarely in the harder, more subjective category, so realistic target benchmarks for a first model iteration are likely in the 60-80% binary-accuracy range rather than 90%+, especially with limited labeled Indian-ad data.
- Fine-tuning a dedicated, relatively small multimodal fusion model on task-specific labeled data (as in SCANNER Alcohol, DEFEND's in-distribution results, and the Pitt Ads persuasion-strategy model) consistently outperforms general-purpose zero-shot VLMs/LLMs on ad-related classification — reinforcing that a custom-trained fusion architecture (not a prompted foundation model alone) should be the primary approach, with an LLM/VLM used only as an auxiliary baseline or weak-labeling tool.

### Gaps
- No existing public benchmark was found for a "promoting vs. preventing addictive substance use" binary task specifically — all reported numbers above are from adjacent but distinct tasks (topic, sentiment, persuasion strategy, logo/brand detection), so there is no direct prior accuracy ceiling/floor for this exact task.
- Did not find reported benchmarks for Indian-language or Indian-market ad datasets specifically; all benchmark numbers above come from English-language / Western datasets (Pitt Ads, CMU-MOSEI, US tobacco/alcohol surveillance studies), which is a meaningful gap given the project's India-specific focus (e.g., Hindi/regional-language speech, India-specific visual/cultural cues, and India's surrogate-advertising regulatory context).

## Are there techniques for handling weak/noisy co-occurring signals (e.g., a mandatory warning disclaimer inside an otherwise promotional ad) — MIL, temporal segment attention, evidence-localization?

### Takeaway
Weakly-supervised temporal action localization (WS-TAL) research, framed as a Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) problem over video segments with only a video-level label, is the most directly transferable toolkit: segment-based MIL (S-MIL) and newer proposal-based MIL (P-MIL) methods learn to attend to/localize the segments that actually drive the video-level label while down-weighting or ignoring irrelevant segments — precisely the mechanism needed to let a model learn that a 3-second disclaimer should not dominate the label for an otherwise 30-second promotional ad.

### Cited Findings
- Weakly-supervised temporal action localization aims to localize and classify actions in untrimmed videos using only video-level labels (not frame/segment-level labels), and is formulated as a Multiple Instance Learning problem where a "bag" of segment instances shares one video-level label. — [Proposal-Based Multiple Instance Learning for Weakly-Supervised Temporal Action Localization, CVPR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17861)
- The standard baseline framework is Segment-based MIL (S-MIL), where segment-level prediction scores are supervised only by the video-level label; a known weakness is that the training objective (segment-level scoring) is inconsistent with the test-time objective (proposal/region-level scoring), leading to suboptimal localization. — [P-MIL, CVPR 2023 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Ren_Proposal-Based_Multiple_Instance_Learning_for_Weakly-Supervised_Temporal_Action_Localization_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- Proposal-based MIL (P-MIL) improves on this with a two-stage pipeline: first train an S-MIL model and generate candidate proposals by thresholding the attention sequence, then classify and aggregate those proposals into video-level classification scores supervised by the video-level label — directly analogous to "find the candidate promotional/preventive segments, then aggregate them into one ad-level stance label." — [P-MIL, arXiv:2305.17861](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17861)
- W-TALC (Weakly-supervised Temporal Activity Localization and Classification) is an earlier, widely-cited MIL-style baseline for this same weak-label video setting. — [arXiv:1807.10418](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.10418)
- Newer work incorporates multimodal large language models as "dual priors" to guide weakly-supervised temporal action localization, suggesting MLLM-based segment scoring/filtering is an emerging alternative to pure attention-based MIL. — [Weakly Supervised Temporal Action Localization via Dual-Prior Collaborative Learning Guided by MLLMs, arXiv:2411.08466](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.08466v2)
- Separately, the SemEval-2020 Task 11 span-identification subtask (see above) provides an NLP-side analogue: identifying which text spans actually carry propaganda/persuasion content within a longer document, rather than labeling the whole document uniformly — a text-domain counterpart to video segment localization. — [SemEval-2020 Task 11](https://aclanthology.org/2020.semeval-1.186/)

### Inferences
- The most directly applicable architecture pattern for the disclaimer-vs-main-message problem is: segment the ad video/transcript into short temporal chunks (e.g., 1-3 second video clips, or sentence-level text spans), score each chunk's "promoting" vs. "preventing" vs. "neutral/disclaimer" evidence with an attention mechanism (S-MIL-style), and aggregate chunk-level scores into a video-level label using an attention-weighted pooling (top-k pooling or learned attention weights) rather than naive averaging — this allows the model to learn to down-weight a short mandatory disclaimer against a longer promotional narrative, exactly as P-MIL down-weights irrelevant background segments relative to true action segments.
- This also suggests an opportunity for interpretability: the per-segment attention weights double as an evidence-localization output, letting the project visualize "here is the part of the ad driving the promoting/preventing classification" — useful for a research paper's qualitative analysis and for regulatory-relevant interpretability (e.g., showing a disclaimer was present but brief/low-salience relative to the main promotional content).
- Combining the video-domain MIL/segment-attention approach with the text-domain span-identification approach (SemEval-2020 Task 11 style) would give a genuinely multimodal evidence-localization pipeline — attention over video segments and attention over transcript spans, potentially fused.

### Gaps
- Did not find any paper applying MIL/WS-TAL techniques specifically to advertisement content or to the disclaimer/main-message separation problem — all MIL/WS-TAL sources found are from the general action-recognition/localization literature (e.g., THUMOS, ActivityNet benchmarks), so applying them to ad-stance classification is a proposed methodological transfer, not a directly cited precedent.
- Did not find literature specifically on "evidence against the majority label" or "adversarial/contradictory segment" handling (e.g., robust aggregation functions beyond simple attention pooling, such as noisy-label-robust MIL variants) within the tool budget for this research pass — flagged as a possible follow-up area if the report writer wants more rigor on robustness to the disclaimer specifically being deliberately conflicting evidence rather than just a low-salience segment.
