# Existing Public Datasets Relevant to an Indian Promotional-vs-Preventive Addiction Ad Dataset

## What is the Pitt Ads Dataset (image and video ads with topic/sentiment/strategy annotations) — exact contents, size, access method, license?

### Takeaway
The Pitt (University of Pittsburgh) Ads Dataset is the closest existing large-scale benchmark for "automatic understanding of advertisements" — it has rich topic/sentiment/persuasion-strategy/action-reason annotations for tens of thousands of image ads and thousands of video ads — but it is US/Western-sourced, has no addiction-product-specific labels, and critically has **no promotional-vs-preventive (PSA vs. marketing) intent label**; it must be requested from the authors by email rather than downloaded directly, and its license is unclear/unspecified.

### Cited Findings
- The dataset consists of 64,832 advertisement images and 3,477 video ads, annotated with ten types of information: topic, sentiment, whether the ad is funny/exciting/effective, the prompted action and the reasoning/argument for it, symbolic associations, metaphorical object transformations, and the climax moment in video ads — introduced in Hussain, Zhang & Kovashka, "Automatic Understanding of Image and Video Advertisements," CVPR 2017 — [paper](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/hussain_zhang_kovashka_ads_cvpr2017.pdf); [ar5iv version](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.03067)
- Each image/question was annotated by 3–5 independent Amazon Mechanical Turk workers, and all individual annotator responses are retained (not just an aggregate) — [Pitt readme](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/ads/readme_images.txt)
- Access is by direct request: "To obtain the dataset for research purposes, please email" the authors; the official project/download page is http://www.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/ads/ — [Pitt Ads homepage](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/ads/)
- Video ads are distributed as a CSV of video URLs (`final_video_id_list.csv`) rather than as files, and the project page lists no explicit license or terms-of-use text beyond an NSF funding disclaimer — [Pitt Ads homepage](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/ads/)
- A mirror exists on Hugging Face ("PittImageVideoAdsDataset," ~64,500 images in Parquet format, plus reference to 3,480 video ads); the Hugging Face listing marks the license as "unknown" — [creative-graphic-design/PittImageVideoAdsDataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/creative-graphic-design/PittImageVideoAdsDataset)
- A follow-on resource built on the same image corpus adds a persuasion-strategy ontology (grounded in social psychology/marketing) with 1–3 strategy labels per sample; "concreteness" is the most-used strategy, followed by "eager" and "fashionable" — Kumar et al., "Persuasion Strategies in Advertisements," AAAI 2023 — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09626); [project site](https://midas-research.github.io/persuasion-advertisements/)
- A 2019 TPAMI follow-up, "Interpreting the Rhetoric of Visual Advertisements" (Ye & Kovashka), extends the symbolism/rhetoric annotations on the same image set — [paper](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~kovashka/ye_kovashka_ads_tpami2019.pdf)
- A 2024 ACL paper, "Don't Buy It! Reassessing the Ad Understanding Abilities of [VLMs]," uses the Pitt Ads benchmark to stress-test modern vision-language models, indicating the dataset remains an active benchmark — [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-short.77.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because topic categories in the public documentation are things like "Electronics," "Cars and automobiles," "Safety," etc. (general product/industry categories), and no "public service announcement" category is listed on the project page, the dataset's topic taxonomy appears oriented toward commercial product categories rather than a promotional/preventive or PSA/marketing binary — this is an inference from the absence of such a category in the available documentation, not a confirmed negative (the full topic list was not directly inspected).
- The lack of a posted license and the "email to request" access model means any use of Pitt Ads as a pretraining source or methodological template for this project should budget time for author contact and should not assume redistribution rights.

### Gaps
- The complete, exact topic-category list (all ~38 categories reportedly used in the original CVPR 2017 paper) was not retrieved verbatim in this research pass — only example categories were visible on the fetched page.
- No information was found confirming whether any alcohol/tobacco-specific topic category exists within Pitt Ads, nor whether any of the 3,477 video ads are themselves anti-tobacco/anti-alcohol PSAs rather than product marketing.
- Could not confirm an explicit SPDX-style license (e.g., CC-BY, research-only, non-commercial) for Pitt Ads beyond the "unknown" tag on the Hugging Face mirror and the absence of license text on the homepage.

## Are there any existing datasets of Indian television/online advertisements (general, or specifically alcohol/tobacco/health-related)?

### Takeaway
There is no known public, annotated dataset of Indian video advertisements labeled for addiction-product promotional content; the only readily available "Indian TV ad" machine-learning datasets are generic commercial-detection/segmentation datasets (not content- or topic-labeled), and tobacco/alcohol-advertising evidence in India exists mainly as academic content-analysis studies (e.g., on pan masala surrogate advertising) rather than as structured, downloadable datasets.

### Cited Findings
- The "TV News Channel Commercial Detection Dataset" (UCI ML Repository / Kaggle) contains standard audio-visual shot features extracted from 150 hours of TV news broadcasts across 3 Indian channels (CNN-IBN, NDTV 24X7, TIMES NOW) and 2 international channels (BBC, CNN), with 129,685 instances and 12 attributes, recorded at 720×576/25fps — this dataset labels shots as commercial vs. non-commercial (binary), not by product/brand/intent — [UCI dataset](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/326/tv+news+channel+commercial+detection+dataset); [Kaggle mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/prashant111/tv-news-channel-commercial-detection-dataset); [related paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.01209)
- A 2005 Indian Journal of Cancer study ("Pan masala advertisements are surrogate for tobacco products") monitored a popular Hindi TV news channel and its English counterpart for 24 hours and analyzed repetition/estimated ad spend of pan masala advertising as tobacco surrogate marketing; it estimated the annual sale value of Pan Parag pan masala at Rs. 67.1 million against an estimated annual two-channel ad spend of Rs. 244.6 million — this is content-analysis research, not a released structured/annotated video dataset — [Indian Journal of Cancer](https://journals.lww.com/indianjcancer/fulltext/2005/42020/pan_masala_advertisements_are_surrogate_for.6.aspx)
- Surrogate advertising (indirect promotion of banned products like tobacco/alcohol via associated brand names) is a well-documented phenomenon in India post the May 1, 2004 tobacco-ad ban, discussed broadly in advocacy and academic literature (Vital Strategies report "Hidden in Plain Sight: Surrogate Marketing of Tobacco") but again not packaged as a labeled dataset — [Vital Strategies PDF](http://www.vitalstrategies.org/wp-content/uploads/TERM_HiddeninPlainSight_December-2022.pdf); [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrogate_advertising)
- The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) publishes structured data on advertising complaints (company, brand, product, complaint type, guideline violated, media type, resolution) — available via Dataful.in and ASCI's own annual/half-yearly complaint reports — but this is a complaints registry, not a corpus of ad video/images, and complaint records do not include the ad video itself — [Dataful.in dataset](https://dataful.in/datasets/20112/); [ASCI reports](https://www.ascionline.in/reports/)

### Inferences
- Because no existing Indian ad dataset carries content/topic labels (only commercial-block detection or complaint metadata), a new project building an Indian promotional-vs-preventive addiction-ad dataset would be filling a genuine, unaddressed gap rather than duplicating prior work.
- The ASCI complaint database could be a useful **seed list** for identifying specific brands/campaigns that were flagged as surrogate/addiction-adjacent advertising (to guide targeted video collection), even though it does not supply the videos themselves.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of any Kaggle/academic-torrent/HuggingFace dataset specifically of Indian alcohol or tobacco advertisements (image or video), labeled or unlabeled.
- Could not confirm whether the TV News Channel Commercial Detection Dataset's underlying video/audio data (not just features) is downloadable, or only derived shot-level features are distributed — this affects whether it could be repurposed, even as unlabeled raw footage, for this project.
- No information was found on whether ASCI maintains or releases an internal archive of the actual ad creative files tied to complaints (likely not, given it is a self-regulatory complaints body, not an archive).

## Are there datasets built for tobacco/alcohol detection in images or social media posts?

### Takeaway
Several labeled image datasets exist for smoking/cigarette detection and for alcohol-related social-media image classification, and these are useful for transfer learning on visual "smoking/drinking act" or "product presence" recognition, but none label promotional intent, advertising strategy, or anything resembling "promotional vs. preventive" messaging — they classify presence/absence of smoking, cigarettes, or alcoholic beverages in otherwise unlabeled-for-intent images.

### Cited Findings
- "Smoker Detection [Image] Classification Dataset" (Kaggle): 1,120 images, balanced 560 "Smoking" / 560 "NotSmoking," images 250×250, curated by search-engine keyword scraping (cigarette smoking, smoker, coughing, inhaler use, drinking water, etc. as negative confounders) — [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/sujaykapadnis/smoking)
- "CigDet" (Cigarette Detection) dataset on Mendeley Data: 557 images (446 train / 111 test), derived from the "Smoking" subset of the above smoker-detection dataset, intended for cigarette-specific object detection — [Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/6hyrr8typ7/1)
- A Roboflow Universe "Cigarette Detection" dataset (Cummins College) contains 1,471 open-source cigarette-smoking images for classification/detection — [Roboflow Universe](https://universe.roboflow.com/cummins-college-5v6r0/cigarrete-detection)
- Academic region-extraction work (arXiv 2103.12523, "Region extraction based approach for cigarette usage classification using deep learning") reports a transfer-learning approach reaching 96.87% accuracy / 97.32% precision / 96.46% recall on smoking vs. not-smoking classification — [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12523)
- For alcohol: an "Instagram alcohol dataset" of ~34K posts has been used in research, further split into a spatial/location-driven subset (19,409 posts) and a demographics-driven subset (11,050 posts) — cited in a content-moderation survey — [arXiv 2411.17123](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.17123)
- "Drinks & Crowds: Characterizing Alcohol Consumption through Crowdsensing and Social Media" (IMWUT 2019) built on a base corpus of ~1.7 million Instagram posts from Switzerland (2010–2016), from which 5,823 alcohol-related posts were curated, combined with a 200+ person smartphone crowdsensing study; the resulting classifier reached 82.3% accuracy on alcohol-category classification and 90% on alcohol/non-alcohol classification — [Idiap PDF](https://idiap.ch/~gatica/publications/PhanMuralidharGatica-imwut19.pdf); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3328930)
- "Identifying substance use risk based on deep neural networks and Instagram social media data" classified substance-use risk (alcohol, tobacco, drug) from 2,287 Instagram users' profiles using a CNN (images) + LSTM (text) ensemble — [Nature/Neuropsychopharmacology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-018-0247-x); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6333814/)
- A separate study hand-annotated 89,917 images to measure the link between Instagram alcohol-post exposure and later alcohol use (exact dataset name/public availability not confirmed in the search snippet) — referenced within the alcohol image-detection literature (see "zero-shot alcohol image detection" paper below)
- "The promise of zero-shot learning for alcohol image detection" (Scientific Reports 2023) compares CLIP-based zero-shot detection against the task-specific "Alcoholic Beverage Identification Deep Learning Algorithm" (ABIDLA2), which screens Facebook/Instagram/Twitter/YouTube/Netflix media for alcohol exposure — [Nature Sci Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-39169-4); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10363523/); ABIDLA2 validation: [Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/acer.14925)
- All of these are image/photo or social-post classification datasets — none target video advertisements, and none were found to carry India-specific content or promotional/preventive-intent labels.

### Inferences
- These datasets (smoking/cigarette image classifiers, ABIDLA2-style alcohol detectors, CLIP zero-shot alcohol detection) could plausibly be reused as **frame-level visual feature detectors** (e.g., "is a cigarette/alcoholic beverage visible in this frame") as an auxiliary signal or weak-supervision source for the new video-ad dataset, even though they don't solve the promotional-vs-preventive classification task directly.
- None of these datasets appear to be specific to Indian products/packaging/branding (e.g., Indian cigarette brands, gutka/pan masala packaging), which is a visual-domain gap — a cigarette or liquor-bottle detector trained on Western imagery may not generalize well to Indian pan masala/gutka sachets or regional liquor branding.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm public download/license terms for the "34K Instagram alcohol dataset" or the "89,917 hand-annotated images" dataset — these were referenced secondhand in review/survey papers rather than located at a primary source with explicit terms.
- No gutka/pan masala-specific image-classification dataset was found (smokeless-tobacco-adjacent product recognition appears to be an unaddressed niche).
- No social-media video (as opposed to still image) dataset specifically for alcohol detection was found in this pass.

## Are there any datasets of anti-tobacco/anti-alcohol PSAs or health campaign videos?

### Takeaway
The most directly relevant and reusable find is the **Public Health Advocacy Dataset (PHAD)** — 5,730 tobacco-related videos from YouTube and TikTok with a permissive CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license and a public download pipeline — but it is US/global-social-media-sourced (not India-specific) and, like every other dataset found, does **not** label promotional vs. preventive intent; beyond PHAD, PSA archives exist (Internet Archive, UCSF Tobacco Control Archives, CDC Media Campaign Resource Center, India's NTCP) but mostly as unstructured media libraries rather than ML-ready labeled datasets.

### Cited Findings
- PHAD (Public Health Advocacy Dataset): 5,730 videos (~4.3 million frames, 12.3 GB), sourced from YouTube (4,014 videos) and TikTok (1,716 videos), covering 8 tobacco product types (cigarettes, e-cigarettes, vaping devices, smokeless tobacco, menthol variants, etc.), with metadata including engagement metrics, descriptions, search keywords/hashtags, and temporal segmentation of key moments; licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; metadata as JSON with a Python download script (via Apify links) for reconstructing the video files — [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13572v1); [project page](https://uark-cviu.github.io/projects/PHAD/)
- PHAD's documentation does not indicate a promotional-vs-preventive (pro-tobacco marketing vs. anti-tobacco public-health messaging) label — it classifies by tobacco product type and usage context, not by message intent — [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13572v1)
- The Internet Archive hosts unstructured historical anti-smoking PSA/commercial compilations, e.g. "Anti-Smoking Spots" and "Assorted Smoking Commercials" (aired in Massachusetts, Colorado, Arizona) — useful as raw PSA footage but with no annotation layer — [Anti-Smoking Spots](https://archive.org/details/tobacco_jny27a00); [Assorted Smoking Commercials](https://archive.org/details/tobacco_psy27a00)
- UCSF Library's "Tobacco Control Archives" maintains an extensive video library including anti-smoking PSAs, news programs, and documentaries, as part of the broader UCSF Industry Documents collections — [UCSF Tobacco Control Archives](https://www.library.ucsf.edu/archives/tobacco/)
- The WHO FCTC maintains a "Health Warnings Database" of pictorial health warnings/messages for tobacco packaging globally (images, not video ads) — referenced in search results though not directly fetched for detail in this pass
- CDC's Media Campaign Resource Center (MCRC) is a searchable catalog of CDC-licensed tobacco counter-advertising (TV, radio, print, out-of-home, and digital/social assets) but access and reuse are gated: "MCRC materials may be used only by nonprofit health-related organizations or government agencies," each requester must sign a use agreement specifying intended use, and costs apply (talent/image/music license fees, plus "theatrical and industrial use" fees for certain venues) — this is not an open research dataset — [MCRC Overview](https://cdc.gov/tobacco/php/multimedia-and-tools/mcrc-overview.html); [Request Ads](https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/php/multimedia-and-tools/request-ads-in-mcrc.html)
- India's National Tobacco Control Programme (NTCP), under the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, hosts an "IEC" (Information, Education, Communication) section with tobacco-control PSAs/video spots, including a 2024 multi-language ("When You Quit") campaign released in 17 Indian languages, a second-hand-smoke "Clinical" PSA, and an October 2025 e-cigarette-danger video as part of a "Tobacco Free Youth Campaign 3.0" — these are individually accessible government PSAs but not packaged as a structured/labeled dataset — [NTCP website](https://ntcp.mohfw.gov.in/); [NTCP IEC section](https://ntcp.mohfw.gov.in/IEC); [Vital Strategies: second-hand smoke campaign](https://www.vitalstrategies.org/india-launches-campaign-warn-dangers-second-hand-smoke/); [Vital Strategies: e-cigarettes campaign](https://www.vitalstrategies.org/resources/india-tobacco-control-the-truth-about-electronic-cigarettes/); [Vital Strategies: "When You Quit"](https://www.vitalstrategies.org/resources/india-tobacco-control-when-you-quit/)

### Inferences
- PHAD is the single most structurally useful existing resource for this project: it demonstrates a workable methodology (metadata + Apify-based reacquisition links + CC BY-NC-SA licensing) for building a legally cleaner, reproducible tobacco-video dataset from YouTube/TikTok, and its "8 tobacco product types" taxonomy could inform a similar product/category taxonomy for an Indian dataset.
- Because PHAD, the Internet Archive PSA compilations, and NTCP's own PSAs are the only tobacco-specific video sources identified with any structure, a promotional-vs-preventive label for this project would have to be added manually/by the project team — no existing dataset provides it as ground truth, confirming the stated gap.
- NTCP's own official PSA channel is a legally clean (government-produced, non-copyright-contested for research/educational secondary use within fair-dealing bounds, though formal permission should still be sought) source of "preventive" class examples specific to India, which could partially solve the preventive-class sourcing problem even though it doesn't solve "promotional" class sourcing (which would need to come from brand/surrogate ad content).

### Gaps
- No PSA/health-campaign dataset — Indian or international — was found that already labels videos as "promotional" vs. "preventive"; this confirms the project's premise that this label is a genuine gap requiring new annotation.
- Could not confirm whether WHO's FCTC Health Warnings Database includes any video content (vs. only static pictorial package-warning images) — this needs direct verification by fetching the WHO FCTC database page.
- No equivalent Indian or international dataset specifically of anti-**alcohol** PSAs (as opposed to tobacco) was found; alcohol-specific PSA archives appear far less represented in the literature than tobacco ones.
- Could not determine whether UCSF's Tobacco Control Archives video materials are downloadable/bulk-accessible for research or only viewable item-by-item through their library interface.

## Are there general video classification/understanding datasets commonly used as pretraining/transfer-learning sources?

### Takeaway
Kinetics (400/600/700-class human action recognition), HowTo100M (136M narrated instructional clips), and VATEX (41.3K videos with 825K bilingual captions) are all standard, large, openly available video-language/action pretraining corpora that could reduce the amount of India-specific labeled ad data this project needs, by providing a strong pretrained visual/temporal backbone (and, for HowTo100M/VATEX, video-text alignment) to fine-tune on a smaller labeled Indian-ad set.

### Cited Findings
- Kinetics is a family of three YouTube-video action-recognition datasets — Kinetics-400, -600, -700 — each clip labeled with one of 400/600/700 human action classes respectively — [Quo Vadis, Action Recognition? (arXiv 1705.07750)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.07750)
- HowTo100M contains more than 136 million video clips with automatically transcribed narration/captions, drawn from 1.2 million YouTube videos spanning ~23,000 activities (cooking, handcrafting, personal care, gardening, fitness, etc.); it is described as "the gold-standard dataset for video-language pre-training methods" — [HowTo100M paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339562553_HowTo100M_Learning_a_Text-Video_Embedding_by_Watching_Hundred_Million_Narrated_Video_Clips)
- VATEX is a large-scale multilingual video-and-language dataset: 41,250 unique videos covering 600 activities, each paired with 10 English and 10 Chinese captions, totaling ~825,000 captions (413K English + 413K Chinese) and over 206,000 English–Chinese parallel-translation pairs; official split is 25,991 train / 3,000 validation / 6,000 public-test videos — [VATEX paper (arXiv 1904.03493)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.03493); [VATEX project site](https://eric-xw.github.io/vatex-website/about.html)
- "VideoMix2M," a pretraining corpus concatenating Kinetics-710, Something-Something-v2, and HowTo100M, is used in recent video-model pretraining pipelines, illustrating that these corpora are commonly combined rather than used in isolation — [cited in transfer-learning literature retrieved via search, exact source paper title not individually confirmed]
- General video-model pretraining literature affirms that spatio-temporal classifiers pretrained on Kinetics transfer well and meaningfully improve downstream video-understanding tasks — [Transfer-learning for video classification: Video Swin Transformer on multiple domains (arXiv 2210.09969)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.09969)

### Inferences
- A practical pipeline for this project would likely use a Kinetics-pretrained (or Kinetics+HowTo100M-pretrained) video backbone (e.g., a Video Swin Transformer, SlowFast, or similar architecture) fine-tuned on the much smaller labeled Indian promotional/preventive ad set, substantially reducing the labeled-data volume needed versus training from scratch.
- None of Kinetics/HowTo100M/VATEX contain advertisement-specific content, Indian-language content at meaningful scale, or any addiction/health-message annotation — their value here is purely as generic visual/temporal (and for HowTo100M/VATEX, vision-language) representation learning sources, not as any kind of label or content source for the target task.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence was gathered on licensing terms for Kinetics, HowTo100M, or VATEX specifically (these are generally research-use, YouTube-derived, and subject to YouTube's ToS similar to other YouTube-sourced datasets — see legal section below — but exact per-dataset license text was not individually fetched in this pass).
- Did not verify whether any of these three datasets contain a meaningful fraction of Indian-language or Indian-context videos that might make them more directly relevant (e.g., Kinetics' geographic/cultural distribution is not well documented in what was retrieved).

## Where can Indian YouTube/social media ad content realistically be sourced, and what are the ToS/legal considerations?

### Takeaway
Realistic sourcing routes include Google's public Ads Transparency Center (which surfaces a verified advertiser's current/recent YouTube video ads, searchable without login), official brand/government YouTube channels, the Internet Archive's advertising collections, and ASCI's complaint database as a brand/campaign discovery tool — but YouTube's own Terms of Service explicitly prohibit automated scraping, and Indian copyright law has no explicit text-and-data-mining/AI-training exception, so any large-scale automated collection of ad video content carries contractual (ToS) risk even where outright copyright infringement risk may be mitigable under narrow "research/private study" fair-dealing provisions.

### Cited Findings
- Google's Ads Transparency Center (adstransparency.google.com) is a free, public, login-free archive of ads served by verified advertisers across Search, Shopping, Display, YouTube, Maps, and Play, searchable by advertiser name, country, date range, and ad format; YouTube itself keeps no separate ad archive — video ads an advertiser runs on YouTube surface inside this same tool under a YouTube platform filter — [Ad Transparency Centers & Libraries guide](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ad-transparency-centers-libraries/); [Google Ads Transparency Center guide](https://www.admapix.com/blog/ad-intelligence/google-ads-transparency-center-guide); [YouTube Ad Library explainer](https://dailyintelservice.com/compare/youtube-ad-library-where-google-actually-keeps-it)
- No India-specific documentation/coverage details for the Ads Transparency Center were found in this search pass; general capability (advertiser/domain search, country/date/format filters) is documented but India-specific nuances (e.g., coverage completeness for Indian advertisers, political/social-issue ad disclosure requirements) were not confirmed — [webtonic.io Google Ads Library explainer](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/google-ads-library)
- YouTube's Terms of Service explicitly ban accessing the service "using any automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers)" — [Is Scraping YouTube Legal? ToS Explained](https://youtubescraperapi.com/blog/is-scraping-youtube-legal)
- Legally, scraping publicly visible YouTube data is generally not a *criminal* matter in major jurisdictions (per the hiQ v. LinkedIn line of reasoning treating ToS violations as a civil/contract matter), but it remains a *breach of contract* that can trigger account termination, IP blocking, and civil liability exposure — [Web Scraping for Research: Legal, Ethical, Institutional, and Scientific Considerations (arXiv 2410.23432)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23432)
- Research/fair-use framing can support scraping copyrighted content for analysis, but does not permit republishing/redistributing the scraped content itself without rightsholder permission — a relevant constraint if this project intends to publicly release a video-ad dataset rather than just internal research annotations — [Is web scraping legal? What the law says](https://hasdata.com/blog/is-web-scraping-legal)
- A concrete precedent: YouTube has objected to its platform's subtitle data being scraped and included in "The Pile" training corpus, illustrating active platform pushback against large-scale unlicensed dataset construction from YouTube content — [The Pile (dataset), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pile_(dataset))
- Under Indian law, text/data-mining-style research use is most plausibly anchored to Section 52(1)(a) of the Copyright Act (fair dealing for "private or personal use, including research," "criticism or review," or "reporting of current events") — [SpicyIP: "Crawl Cautiously" series](https://spicyip.com/2020/06/crawl-cautiously-examining-the-legal-landscape-for-text-and-data-mining-in-india-part-i.html)
- However, India has no explicit statutory TDM/AI-training exception (unlike the EU); fair dealing has historically been interpreted narrowly by Indian courts around its enumerated purposes, and the "research" exception is understood as covering human research/private study, not necessarily automated machine-scale mining — [SpicyIP: "Should Indian Copyright Law Prevent Text and Data Mining?"](https://spicyip.com/2019/08/should-indian-copyright-law-prevent-text-and-data-mining.html); [Law School Policy Review: "Training AI, Testing Law"](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2025/08/08/training-ai-testing-law-indias-copyright-challenge-with-tdm/)
- Proposed (not yet adopted) reform directions in Indian legal commentary include a non-commercial research TDM exception combined with lawful-access requirements, rights-reservation mechanisms, and transparency/licensing obligations — signaling the current law is genuinely unsettled rather than clearly permissive or clearly prohibitive — [Law School Policy Review](https://lawschoolpolicyreview.com/2025/08/08/training-ai-testing-law-indias-copyright-challenge-with-tdm/)
- The Internet Archive hosts historical advertising collections (including the tobacco-ad compilations cited in the PSA section above) under its own terms, which are generally more permissive for research reuse than YouTube's ToS, since Internet Archive's mission is explicitly archival/research-oriented — [Internet Archive tobacco collections](https://archive.org/details/tobacco_jny27a00)
- ASCI's structured complaints dataset (Dataful.in) can serve as a discovery/seed-list tool — identifying brand names, campaign timeframes, and product categories (including any tobacco/alcohol-surrogate complaints) that a researcher could then manually search for on official brand YouTube channels, rather than as a source of video files itself — [Dataful.in](https://dataful.in/datasets/20112/)
- YouTube's official Data API v3 is the compliant, ToS-respecting path to collecting *metadata* (titles, descriptions, view counts, channel info, etc.) at scale, though it does not provide a compliant path to bulk video *file* download — [DEV Community: "How to Legally Scrape YouTube Videos Using the YouTube Data API"](https://dev.to/pavithran_25/how-to-legally-scrape-youtube-videos-using-the-youtube-data-api-5bk0)

### Inferences
- A defensible, lower-legal-risk collection strategy for this project would combine: (1) manual/curated collection of a bounded set of ad videos from official brand/government YouTube channels (direct downloads of specifically identified, individually reviewed videos, not bulk automated scraping) rather than broad automated crawling; (2) use of the YouTube Data API for metadata only; (3) leaning on PHAD's precedent (CC BY-NC-SA, Apify-based reacquisition links, metadata-first distribution) as a template for how to distribute the resulting dataset without redistributing copyrighted video files directly; and (4) treating the Ads Transparency Center and ASCI complaints data as discovery tools to identify which specific ads/brands to target, rather than as bulk data sources themselves.
- Because Indian copyright law's research exception is narrowly and ambiguously defined for automated/machine-scale use, and YouTube's ToS flatly prohibits scraping regardless of jurisdiction, this project's legal risk is primarily contractual/platform-level (ToS breach consequences: takedown, account action) rather than a clear criminal/civil copyright violation — but the ambiguity itself is a documented, citable risk factor that should be flagged to the research team and likely warrants an institutional/legal review before any public dataset release.

### Gaps
